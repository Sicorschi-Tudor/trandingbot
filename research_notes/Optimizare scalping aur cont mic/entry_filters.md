# Entry filters for intraday trend-pullback / breakout-retest scalping on gold (XAUUSD) and liquid FX, M1–M15

Scope note for the writer: I searched MQL5 articles/blogs/forum, Oxford Strat, Quantified Strategies, TradingView research scripts, Bulkowski (ThePatternSite), Reddit backtest write-ups, and the academic literature (Park & Irwin; Neely/Weller/Dittmar; Hsu/Taylor/Wang; Batten et al.). The single most important meta-finding: **there is almost no published, cost-inclusive, with-vs-without backtest of any entry filter on XAUUSD at M1–M5.** The best controlled evidence comes from (a) H1 CFD/futures studies, (b) daily-bar futures portfolios, and (c) a few 2026 MQL5 write-ups on gold H1 with partial numbers. Everything below is labelled **[measured]** (numbers from a described test), **[vendor claim]** (self-reported EA/indicator statistics without methodology), or **[opinion]**.

Reminder of our own filter under review: EMA9−EMA21 gap on M5 ≥ 0.4 × ATR14(M1). I found **no external test of an EMA-gap-in-ATR threshold** anywhere; the nearest analogues are ADX gates and dual-EMA alignment filters (see KQ1).

---

## KQ1 — Which trend-strength filters (ADX > 20/25/30, EMA slope, EMA separation in ATR) have backtested evidence of improving pullback entries on gold/FX M1–M15, and by how much?

### Takeaway
Controlled tests mostly show that a **directional** trend-strength gate (ADX above a level, price vs EMA) reduces trade count far more than it improves per-trade expectancy; two independent long-horizon studies rate ADX as adding no value to trend entries, while one gold-H1 developer found a **direction-agnostic daily ADX gate at low thresholds (15–22)** was the only filter that cut drawdown, and that ADX 25 overfit. No test of an EMA-gap-in-ATR gate exists; the closest analogue (dual-EMA fast-above-slow, ratio ~3–4:1) was the most robust of eight MA filters in a 1,000-strategy NASDAQ H1 sweep.

### Cited Findings

**ADX — long-horizon, cost-inclusive tests (not gold-specific)**
- Oxford Strat tested a Donchian breakout system (entry channel 50, exit 25, 6×ATR(20) stop) on 42 futures markets 1980–2014 with an ADX filter "trade only when ADX[i−1] ≥ threshold", sweeping ADX lookback 10–60 and threshold 0–35, with $0 and $100 round-turn cost cases. Conclusion verbatim: "The Average Directional Index (ADX) does not add value to the base case trend-following model." Rated D on an A–D scale. Numeric values are only in charts. — [Oxford Strat, ADX filter (threshold & lookback)](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=11043) [measured]
- A second Oxford Strat study sweeps Donchian entry length 5–200 with ADX(14) threshold 0–60 (step 2), same 42-market 1980–2014 portfolio, same conclusion ("does not add value"), rated D. — [Oxford Strat, Donchian + ADX threshold](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=10998) [measured]
- Alpha Algo Trading Research: NQ futures, 240-min bars, 20-day breakout base, ADX(14) filter variants (above 25; rising; above 20; above 30; above 20 and rising). Author states the basic ADX filter "improved the baseline by 28%" (metric unspecified) and a 20-filter optimisation grid improved return-on-risk by 122.32%; period and costs not stated; underlying trade stats only in images. — [Alpha Algo Trading Research](https://algotr.substack.com/p/stop-trading-in-chop-start-using) [measured but incompletely reported; heavy optimisation]

**ADX — gold-specific**
- MQL5 gold H1 EA developer ("Apex Helios"), XAUUSD H1, in-sample 2023–2024, out-of-sample 2025–2026, 485 trades Jan 2023–Aug 2026, costs not described: a **direction-agnostic daily ADX regime filter** at thresholds 15, 18, 20 and 22 "all improved results"; combined with other changes PF went 1.21 → 1.68 and max DD 21.8% → 11.6%; author says it "did more to reduce drawdown than anything else". **ADX threshold 25**: out-of-sample PF 3.36 but "went negative in-sample" → rejected as overfit. — [MQL5 blog 773874](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/773874) [measured, partial numbers]
- MQL5 "TrendCandleEA", XAUUSD **M30**, 2026-01-01 → 2026-09-09, 100% real ticks (58.3M ticks), Tickmill demo, 0.01 lot, 1:1 TP/SL with SL buffer 1000 points, max spread input 60 points, commission not stated. Filter stack includes ADX(7) ≥ 25, RSI(14) >65/<35, MACD momentum, BB(200,0.5) touch on M30 + BB-200 slope on H3, ZigZag(24,5,3), "virgin price" lookback. Results: Config "Balanced" (lookback 1000): 98 trades, WR 72.45%, PF 2.65, max balance DD 6.55%, 62 long/36 short; Config "Selective" (lookback 10000): 37 trades, WR 83.78%, PF 5.60, DD 4.25%, 37 long/0 short. The article attributes the 100% long bias to the ZigZag filter, not ADX. No ADX on/off comparison; test period overlaps development period and is a strong gold bull year. — [MQL5 blog 775609](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/775609) [measured, in-sample, single filter stack]
- US_TECH100 (Nasdaq CFD) H1, Apr 2021–Apr 2026, 29,646 broker bars, MACD(12,26,9) histogram cross entries, SL 2.5×ATR(14), TP 3.0×ATR(14), 48-bar time stop, no costs (author estimates ~0.05R/trade spread): a **regime filter defined by ADX (trend >25, range <20) plus 20th/80th-percentile ATR(500 bars)** reduced trades 2,213 → 1,634 and left expectancy unchanged (+0.017R → +0.013R), PF 1.03 → 1.02, DD −28.8R → −33.4R. Raw results by regime: TREND (ADX>25) 1,199 trades, WR 45.6%, +0.005R; NORMAL 435 trades, WR 47.4%, +0.036R; RANGE (ADX<20) 78 trades, WR 42.3%, **−0.069R**; VOLATILE 501 trades, WR 48.5%, +0.042R. — [MQL5 article 22290](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/22290) [measured, index CFD not gold]

**Regime/trend filters across many symbols (equities, daily)**
- Reddit backtest, 15 symbols on 5 exchanges, post-2000 daily data, candlestick entries with fixed stop/target: Blind 3,015 trades, avg +0.48%/trade, risk ratio 0.188; Regime-filtered 784 trades (−74%), +0.46%, 0.178; +Volume 599 trades, +0.43%, 0.166; **+ADX 356 trades (−88%), +0.35%, 0.129** — i.e., each additional gate including ADX *lowered* average return per trade. Win rate 32.8% → 32.1%. Per-symbol: filter helped AAPL (0.358→0.509), META, NVDA, SIE; did nothing or hurt on MSFT, CBA, 0700.HK. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1skdizm/snapshots/2026-04-13T15%3A22%3A00.465913Z) [measured, equities daily]

**EMA alignment / EMA separation (analogue to our EMA9−EMA21 ≥ 0.4 ATR)**
- BreakoutOS sweep: NASDAQ 60-min long breakout base (prev-day low ± 0.8×ATR(40), EOD exit), 1,000 sibling strategies × 1,000 market conditions, period/costs not stated. Single SMA close-above filter (10–200): 540/1,000 strategies improved net-profit/DD, avg trade +48%, viability 83%, and **SMA length barely mattered (20/50/100/200 all ≈ +50% avg trade)**. Single EMA filters: viability ~50%, "no consistent pattern". **Dual-EMA fast-above-slow was the top condition with the broadest, most stable optimisation map; sweet spot fast 10–20 / slow 70–90 (ratio ≈ 3:1–4:1).** No win-rate/PF reported. — [BreakoutOS MA filter test](https://breakoutos-com.pages.dev/blog/moving-average-filter-tested-on-1000-strategies) [measured, index H1]
- An ATR-normalised EMA-spread indicator ("EMA Spread Strength HTF (ATR-Normalized)") exists on TradingView — fast/slow EMA gap divided by ATR — but ships no performance results. — [TradingView script listing](https://tr.tradingview.com/scripts/movingaverage/page-36/?script_access=all) [no data]
- MQL5 free EA "ADX Trend Pullback EA": defaults ADX ≥ 25 **and rising**, EMA pullback entry at **0.5 ATR from the EMA**, ATR stops/targets; no performance figures published. — [MQL5 code 73958](https://www.mql5.com/en/code/73958) [no data; useful default parameters]
- Raschke "Holy Grail" as coded in MQL5 (Oct 2026): ADX(14) ≥ 30 and rising two bars, EMA20 sloping with the trade, pullback touches EMA20, **only the first pullback per ADX cycle**, exit when ADX < 20 or close crosses EMA against trade; recommended EURUSD H4 with an estimated 10–20 signals/year; article explicitly reports **no backtest**. — [MQL5 article 24280](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/24280) [rules only]

**Academic context (do technical rules work at all on gold/FX?)**
- Batten, Lucey, McGroarty, Peat & Urquhart (JIFMIM 52, 2018): intraday gold and silver, three moving-average rule families. With standard literature parameters the rules offer "no predictive power whatsoever"; across a wider parameter universe some combinations show significant predictability **in gold only** (none in silver); **longer parameter settings performed better than traditional ones**. Transaction costs not mentioned on the abstract page. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfin/v52y2018icp102-113.html) [measured, academic]
- Hsu, Taylor & Wang (J. Int. Econ. 102, 2016): 30 currencies, 45 years daily, >21,000 rules, stepwise data-snooping test; find excess profitability in developed and emerging currencies, cross-validated out-of-sample, but effectiveness diminishes over time and is strongest in emerging markets. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:inecon:v:102:y:2016:i:c:p:188-208) [measured, academic, daily]
- Neely, Weller & Dittmar (JFQA 1997): genetic-programming rules on six exchange rates 1981–1995 produce "economically significant out-of-sample excess returns" not explained by systematic risk. — [GP bibliography](https://gpbib.pmacs.upenn.edu/gp-html/neely_1997_JFQA.html) [measured, academic, daily]
- Park & Irwin (2007 survey, secondary citation): 59% of modern studies positive, 21% negative, 20% mixed; their 2010 futures "reality check" (1985–2004) finds technical profits declined after the early 1990s. — [Park & Irwin review (mirror)](https://xxxxyyyy80008.github.io/docs/time_series/technical_analysis/ta_rev1.html); [Park & Irwin 2010](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jfutmk:v:30:y:2010:i:7:p:633-659) [academic, daily]

### Inferences
- The two cleanest cost-inclusive tests (Oxford Strat, 42 markets × 34 years) say ADX gates do **not** add value to breakout/trend entries; the gold-H1 developer's experience agrees for ADX 25 (overfit) but found **low-threshold (15–22), direction-agnostic** ADX useful as a drawdown reducer. If we test ADX at all, test it as a *range-avoidance* gate at 15–22 rather than a trend-confirmation gate at 25–30.
- The MACD/TECH100 by-regime table is the most useful quantitative anchor: ADX<20 ("RANGE") was the **only** negative-expectancy regime (−0.069R, 42% WR) but comprised only 78/2,213 trades (3.5%). A chop filter's value is bounded by how many trades actually fall in the chop bucket — it cannot rescue a strategy whose edge is thin everywhere.
- For our EMA9−EMA21 ≥ 0.4×ATR gap: the BreakoutOS result that **dual-EMA alignment** is the most robust MA filter (with a ~3–4:1 period ratio; 9/21 is 2.3:1) is indirect support for the *direction* component; the ATR-normalised *magnitude* threshold has no external evidence either way and should be treated as an in-house hypothesis to be validated by bucketing our own trades by gap size (e.g., <0.2, 0.2–0.4, 0.4–0.8, >0.8 ATR).
- The academic literature supports that gold *does* have some intraday MA-rule predictability, but with **longer** parameters than the classic short ones — a caution against very fast EMAs (9/21) on M1/M5 as the sole trend definition.

### Gaps
- No published XAUUSD M1–M5 backtest compares ADX 20 vs 25 vs 30 on the same pullback logic with costs. The only M5/M15 ADX gold items are vendor listings (e.g., GoldMaster v3 M5 PF 1.69 Jul 2025–Jun 2026 and 1.28 Oct 2025–Oct 2026, no filter-off run) — [EarnForex forum](https://www.earnforex.com/forum/threads/goldmaster-v3-expert-advisor-%E2%80%93-updated-xauusd-trading-strategy-with-adx-filter.55734/) [vendor claim].
- No test of EMA *slope* thresholds (in ATR or %) was found anywhere.
- No test of EMA *gap in ATR units* was found; Oxford Strat's Efficiency-Ratio sensitivity charts (see KQ2) are the only parametric "trend-strength threshold" sweep, and their numbers are image-only.

## KQ2 — Which chop/range detectors (Choppiness Index, BB-width percentile, ATR percentile, Efficiency Ratio) reliably identify conditions where pullback entries fail, and at what thresholds?

### Takeaway
Thresholds in circulation (CHOP > 61.8 = choppy / < 38.2 = trending; ER > 0.6; BB-width below 10–30th percentile; ATR below 20th / above 80th percentile) are **conventions, not tested optima**: every source I could open either had results behind a paywall or in unreadable charts, or published rules without numbers. The only quantified evidence that a "range" bucket loses money is the TECH100 ADX<20 bucket (−0.069R) and the Apex Helios daily-ADX result above; for CHOP, BB-width and ER I found zero measured with/without comparisons on any instrument.

### Cited Findings
- Choppiness Index conventions: lookback 14; >61.8 choppy/ranging, <38.2 trending; the indicator is directionless, so it is a filter not a signal. — [BabyPips CHOP lesson](https://www.babypips.com/learn/forex/choppiness-index-chop) [convention]; LuxAlgo notes the index "summarizes the whole lookback and reacts with a delay" — [LuxAlgo CHOP](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/choppiness-index/) [opinion]
- Quantified Strategies ran a CHOP backtest on SPY; rules and all statistics are paywalled/in images; nothing extractable. — [Quantified Strategies CHOP](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/choppiness-index-trading-strategy) [measured, inaccessible]
- FMZ "ADX + CI dual-confirmed MA crossover" uses CHOP threshold 50 as the filter, backtest 2024-02-22 → 2024-12-06 on ETH/USDT; no results in the text. — [FMZ strategy 483023](https://www.fmz.com/lang/en/strategy/483023) [rules only]
- Kaufman Efficiency Ratio: Oxford Strat's KAMA study (42 futures, 1980–2011, $0 and $100 RT costs) sweeps ER length 2–100 and fast-MA 2–28 and reports PF, Sharpe, UPI, CAGR, DD, %profitable as 3-D surfaces only; filter level fixed at 0.01; overall rating **C**. — [Oxford Strat KAMA](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=5282) [measured, image-only]
- A community ER gist thresholds entry at ER > 0.6 on SPY and explicitly "does not include considerations such as transaction costs or slippage". — [GitHub gist](https://gist.github.com/18182324/bc1e9aa66dc2f32788b1df13af77ffae) [illustrative]
- A TradingView ER-strategy author notes that "through backtests, noticed better results when **not** using" the ER confirmation check. — [TradingView Kaufman ER strategy](https://tradingview.com/script/WRhJ1RJb-Kaufman-s-Efficiency-Ratio-Strategy-KL) [anecdote]
- Bollinger-width percentile: open-source "Squeeze Ping" scripts flag quiet periods when BB width is in a low percentile; author suggests ~10% for rare squeezes, 20–30% for more setups; **no performance results** on any script found. — [TradingView Squeeze Ping v1.5](https://in.tradingview.com/script/FWvj6Be3-Squeeze-Ping-BB-Width-Percentile-quiet-expansion-v1-5) [convention]
- ATR percentile regime buckets in circulation: 100-bar window, LOW <25th, NORMAL 25–50th, HIGH 50–75th, EXTREME >75th; suggested rule "avoid new entries in EXTREME". — [TradingView volatility scripts](https://tr.tradingview.com/scripts/volatility/page-10) [convention]. LuxAlgo warns that **intraday "volatility follows the clock"** (session opens/closes), so an ATR rank cycles with time of day and should be read from a higher timeframe or a session-aware baseline. — [LuxAlgo Volatility Switch](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/volatility-switch.md) [opinion]
- The only quantified chop bucket: TECH100 H1 MACD, RANGE regime (ADX<20) 78 trades, WR 42.3%, −0.069R vs +0.017R overall; adding the regime filter (which also excluded the profitable VOLATILE bucket) did not improve PF (1.03 → 1.02). — [MQL5 article 22290](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/22290) [measured]
- Gold-specific range filter that *was* kept after testing: London/Asian-range breakout on XAUUSD, Jan 2023–Sep 2026 real ticks, skip the day if the 03:00–08:00 range is **< 0.30% or > 1.60% of price**; final system PF 1.37–1.38 on two brokers, 715 trades, ~49% WR, max equity DD 17–19% at 1% risk; 2026 unseen data PF 1.61 (157 trades). Author says "no range filter" produced false breakouts from narrow ranges and late moves from wide ones, but gives no before/after numbers. — [MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540) [measured; filter effect qualitative]

### Inferences
- For a scalper that already requires a trend (EMA alignment), a chop detector mostly double-counts the trend filter; the evidence (TECH100, Reddit 15-symbol, Oxford Strat) is consistent that stacked regime filters cut trades ~25–75% while leaving per-trade expectancy flat or lower.
- The cheapest robust "range" definition with any measured support is **low ADX (<~20) or a low daily ADX**, not CHOP/BB-width. If we adopt a chop gate, define it on a slower timeframe (M15/H1) so it doesn't oscillate with the intraday volatility clock.
- The gold range-breakout result suggests a two-sided *range-width* filter in % of price (≈0.30–1.60% of the Asian range) has practical value for gold; for a pullback scalper the analogue would be bucketing results by the preceding N-hour range in ATR or % terms.

### Gaps
- No measured with/without result for Choppiness Index, BB-width percentile or Efficiency Ratio on any FX/gold intraday series. Paywalled (Quantified Strategies) and image-only (Oxford Strat) sources may contain numbers but could not be read.
- No source established at what CHOP/ER level a *pullback* (as opposed to breakout) entry's win rate degrades.

## KQ3 — Is there evidence on optimal pullback depth (38–62% retracement; EMA20 vs EMA50 touch) and on "first pullback after breakout" vs later pullbacks? What about breakout-retest confirmation?

### Takeaway
The Fibonacci depth levels have **no empirical support** (large-sample stock backtests show 38/50/62% retracements are no more frequent than other depths). The best measured material is Bulkowski's (US daily stocks, 9,372 patterns): a retest that **holds the breakout level** outperforms one that closes back through it by a wide margin (41.3% vs 26.7% post-throwback rise; 25.1% vs 17.3% post-pullback decline), and a 2026 intraday NIFTY study (1m–1h) reproduces this: **clean retest +0.31R vs deep retest −0.04R**, while "wait for the retest" as a policy is no better than taking the break (+0.06R vs +0.09R) because only ~47–53% of breaks ever retest. No study quantifies first vs second/third pullback.

### Cited Findings
**Pullback depth**
- "Extensive backtests of Fibonacci retracement over thousands of stocks have shown that the retracements values of 38%, 50%, and 62% had been no likelier to appear than any other of the possible retracement values." — [Wikipedia, Fibonacci retracement](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Fibonacci_retracement) [measured, secondary]
- A frequently cited "Harford & Calloway 2014, 8,200 S&P pullbacks" study could not be located and should be treated as unverified. — [Acadifi forum](https://www.acadifi.com/community/fibonacci-retracement-levels-support-resistance-trading) [unverified]
- No source compares EMA20-touch vs EMA50-touch pullbacks; one guide states "there is no reliable published win rate for this exact tag, close, and swing-stop rule". — [The Right Trader, EMA pullback](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trading-strategies/ema-pullback-strategy) [opinion]
- Default pullback-distance parameter in the MQL5 ADX Trend Pullback EA: **0.5 ATR from the EMA**. — [MQL5 code 73958](https://www.mql5.com/en/code/73958) [parameter only]

**Depth relative to the breakout level (Bulkowski, US stocks, Jul 1991–Jun 2008, 9,372 patterns in 841 stocks)**
- Throwbacks (after upward breakouts) that **do not drop below the breakout price**: average subsequent rise 41.3% (n=1,717); those that **do** drop below: 26.7% (n=3,311). Pullbacks (after downward breakouts) that stop short of the breakout price: average decline 25.1% (n=1,355); those that climb above the breakout-day high: 17.3% (n=2,989). — [ThePatternSite, Throwbacks & Pullbacks](https://thepatternsite.com/ThrowPull.html) [measured, daily stocks]
- Stop test on 5,188 throwbacks: stop 2¢ below the breakout-day low then switch to a volatility stop yielded $165,148 gross vs $148,500 for the volatility stop alone (~+11%). — same source [measured]
- Secondary Bulkowski figures (quoted in a book excerpt, not verified against primary): after an upward breakout that "runs in a straight line", a throwback followed in 152/196 cases (78%); after downward breakouts a pullback followed in 104/150 (69%). — [ThePatternSite trends page](https://www.thepatternsite.com/tptrends.html) [secondary]

**Break vs retest, intraday (NIFTY futures, 1m/3m/5m/15m/1h, in-sample, no costs)**
- Breaks that ever retested: 47.0% overall (194/412); by timeframe 1m 49.4%, 3m 53.3%, 5m 53.4%, 15m 51.0%, 1h 50.0%. After a run of >3 closes only 31.2% retested vs 55.8% otherwise. False-break rate overall 13.3% (1m 29.6%, 5m 14.0%, 15m 12.7%).
- Expectancy at a fixed R target: **break entry +0.09R** (n=412) vs control +0.02R; **clean retest** (touched level, never closed through) **+0.31R** (n=118); **deep retest** (closed through, then recovered) **−0.04R** (n=76); clean vs deep t = 3.41. Policy "wait for retest" = P(retest)×E[R|retest] = +0.06R vs +0.09R for taking every break → verdict "no difference". Volume-confirmed breaks +0.11R vs +0.06R (t = 0.82, n.s.). Author caveat: costs excluded and "would likely hurt the break entry more than the retest entry". — [TradingView Break-vs-Retest research script](https://fr.tradingview.com/script/SA4YoyNF-Breakout-Retest-Entry-Signals-the-Break-vs-Retest/) [measured, in-sample, Indian index futures]
- BabyPips forum thread "Is break through and retest better than break through?" contains opinions only, no data. — [BabyPips forum](https://forums.babypips.com/t/is-break-through-and-retest-better-than-break-through/724874) [opinion]

**First pullback**
- "There is no reliable published win rate for buying every first pause after a breakout." — [The Right Trader, first pullback](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trend-analysis/first-pullback) [opinion]
- Raschke's Holy Grail rule as coded: "Only the first pullback per ADX cycle is taken" (ADX ≥ 30 rising, EMA20 touch) — rule, no test. — [MQL5 article 24280](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/24280)
- A trader's informal study of ~150 bases reports a "failed breakout pullback" to the 21-day EMA with a 55% success rate — single unverified sample. — [Amphibian Trading](https://amphibiantrading.substack.com/p/the-failed-breakout-pullback) [anecdote]
- Breakout failure base rates conflict: a Brooks course forum cites the rule of thumb "80% of breakouts fail", while the instructor's slide suggests ~50% after a long range. — [Brooks Trading Course forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/12-market-cycle/breakout-fail/) [opinion]

### Inferences
- The actionable, twice-replicated depth rule is **not a Fibonacci ratio but "did the pullback close back through the breakout/EMA level?"**: a pullback that holds the level (clean) is worth ~+0.3R; one that closes through and comes back is roughly zero-expectancy. For an EMA-pullback scalper the natural invalidation is a **close** beyond the slow EMA (EMA21 on M5), not a wick touch.
- The NIFTY data also imply **not** to require a retest as a precondition: roughly half of valid breaks never retest, so requiring one halves opportunity without raising policy expectancy; instead, if a retest *does* occur, grade it clean vs deep.
- Because retest frequency drops to ~31% after a >3-bar run, a pullback entry after an extended impulse is rarer and the first-pullback rule (Raschke) is a reasonable heuristic — but it is untested.

### Gaps
- No quantified first vs second vs third pullback statistics on any instrument.
- No EMA20 vs EMA50 touch comparison. No gold-specific retest study; the NIFTY study is a single-instrument in-sample panel with unknown period.
- Bulkowski's magnitudes are daily-stock percentages and cannot be translated to R on gold M1; only the *ordering* (hold-level > close-through) should be carried over.

## KQ4 — Do tick-volume or spread filters help on gold CFDs?

### Takeaway
Tick volume is a validated proxy for real FX volume at **hourly** resolution (Pearson 0.96–0.99 on EBS/Hotspot data), but no study shows a tick-volume gate improves gold scalping, and the one intraday break/retest test found volume confirmation not significant (+0.11R vs +0.06R, t = 0.82). Spread filters are universally recommended for gold and the strongest measured evidence is indirect: a tick-level XAUUSD study found "almost every micro edge … was smaller than the spread" (~10–11 points), and the one filter that materially helped the TECH100 H1 strategy was a **session filter** (PF 1.01 → 1.20, DD −29.6R → −9.7R) attributed to spread/liquidity. No with/without max-spread backtest on gold was found.

### Cited Findings
**Tick volume validity**
- Marney (FX Trader Magazine, Apr–Jun 2011): actual traded volume from Hotspot and EBS vs eSignal price-update counts, hourly buckets, EURUSD/USDJPY/GBPUSD/EURCHF. Pearson r: **EURUSD 0.9683 (r² 93.8%), USDJPY 0.9583 (91.8%), GBPUSD 0.9894 (97.9%), EURCHF 0.9714 (94%)**. The author lists reasons the proxy can fail bar-by-bar (news moves without volume; large block trades without price change; range inflates tick count). — [Marney, tick vs real volume PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/145/fxvolume_tick_volume_vs_real_volume_study_1.pdf) [measured, hourly aggregates, 2011]
- A broker-education source argues tick volume "sometimes works poorly" because brokers aggregate different liquidity providers, so tick counts differ across brokers. — [Finance Magnates directory](https://directory.financemagnates.com/view-news/what-is-forex-trade-volume-/15028) [opinion]

**Tick/volume filters — measured effect**
- NIFTY intraday break/retest panel: volume-on breaks +0.11R (n=246) vs volume-off +0.06R (n=166), t = 0.82, "no significant difference". — [TradingView Break-vs-Retest](https://fr.tradingview.com/script/SA4YoyNF-Breakout-Retest-Entry-Signals-the-Break-vs-Retest/) [measured]
- Reddit 15-symbol equity test: adding volume confirmation on top of a regime filter cut trades 784 → 599 and lowered avg return/trade 0.46% → 0.43%. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1skdizm/snapshots/2026-04-13T15%3A22%3A00.465913Z) [measured, daily equities]
- MQL5 XAUUSD tick-level research (Oct 2026): patterns measured = consecutive same-direction ticks, spikes, pauses after spikes; typical spread on the author's broker ~10–11 points; "Almost every micro edge I found was smaller than the spread"; only post-spike continuation survived costs and only with ≤ ~50 ms execution; **no tick-volume finding reported**; author declined to release a tick scalper and recommends timeframes where the typical move is "many times larger than the spread". — [MQL5 blog 777925](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777925) [measured, qualitative]

**Spread filters on gold**
- MQL5 "A Year of Real-Tick Testing on Gold": gold spreads vary "several-fold" between brokers and across the day; thin take-profit scalps are hurt when spread and slippage consume the edge; sharp intraday reversals punish tight-stop breakout entries; no numbers. — [MQL5 blog 774275](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/774275) [opinion from testing]
- Vendor listing for a 1-minute gold scalper: default max spread **30 points** (range 15–50) while "backtest used 100 points" — a mismatch that makes live and backtest incomparable. Another gold EA documents a spread limit default of 300 points, and one breakout EA skips entries when **spread > 10% of the stop distance**. — [MQL5 product 174156](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/174156); [GoldPlaybook EA](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/187655) [vendor parameters]
- "Gold Tick Scalper" uses "Max Spread % of TP = 25" (skip when spread > 25% of take-profit). — [MQL5 product 185226](https://www.mql5.com/fr/market/product/185226) [vendor parameter]
- MQL5 "Best Trading Hours for EAs (and When to Avoid Gold)": qualitative only — prefer early/mid London, early/mid NY and the overlap; avoid late NY, rollover, session transitions, news; suggests a spread filter "If spread > X" with X undefined; page carries affiliate links. — [MQL5 blog 766455](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/766455) [opinion]
- Practitioner spread figures from the session search: ~1.5–2.5 pips during the London–NY overlap on an ECN vs 4–7 pips in the Asian pre-session; EAs hitting consecutive stops in the first hour of London open — [dev.to XAUUSD sessions](https://dev.to/xauusdrobot/when-does-gold-actually-move-xauusd-trading-sessions-explained-i4a) [unverified]
- Session filter as the only effective filter: TECH100 H1 MACD, restricting signals to 14:00–20:00 (NYSE hours) on top of regime+HTF filters: trades 794 → 195, WR 45.8% → 50.3%, expectancy +0.007R → **+0.100R**, PF 1.01 → **1.20**, max DD −29.6R → **−9.7R**; author attributes it to thinner liquidity and wider spreads outside US hours; window chosen a priori. Author also estimates spread friction ≈ 0.05R/trade (≈110R over 2,213 trades) which alone would make the raw strategy unviable. — [MQL5 article 22290](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/22290) [measured]
- Counter-evidence that session edges are unstable: a 5-year gold EA test (2016–2020) found the Asian session poor in 2016–17 and good in 2018–20, London and London+Asian "random/bad", 24h good in 2016; repliers suggest the differences may be backtest noise. — [MQL5 forum 373024](https://mql5.com/en/forum/373024) [measured, qualitative]
- Gold spreads widen around 21:00–22:00 UTC and the 20:00–01:00 UTC window "tends to hurt performance" (practitioner, from the tick-volume search; the MT5 tester "does not accurately model" live spread during news/rollover). — [MQL5 blog 770479](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770479) [opinion]

### Inferences
- A **spread-relative-to-target** gate (skip when spread > ~10–25% of TP or SL, as several EAs do) is more defensible than a fixed point cap for a strategy whose TP/SL scale with ATR; the tick-level study implies the TP must be a multiple (not a fraction) of the spread for any M1 edge to survive.
- Tick volume on MT5 is a trustworthy *hourly* activity proxy but noisy bar-to-bar; the only measured tests of volume *confirmation* show no significant benefit. If used at all, use it as a liquidity/session proxy (e.g., skip bars whose tick count is in the bottom decile for that hour) rather than as entry confirmation.
- Given that session/liquidity was the single filter with a large measured effect in the one controlled CFD study, hour-of-day expectancy on our own trade log is likely a higher-value analysis than any indicator-based chop filter.

### Gaps
- No with/without max-spread backtest on XAUUSD at any level (30/50/100 points) was found; vendor defaults (30, 60, 100, 300 points) are not comparable because point definitions and brokers differ.
- No gold-specific tick-volume filter test. Marney's correlations are for FX majors, 2011, hourly; no M1 or gold figures exist.
- No published hour-of-day expectancy table for gold scalping with variable spreads.

## KQ5 — Does higher-timeframe (M15/H1) alignment help or hurt M1 pullback entries? (Our own data: against-M15 entries did not lose more.)

### Takeaway
Our observation matches the only two gold/CFD studies with numbers: a directional **daily-EMA** trend filter made a gold H1 breakout EA **worse** (in-sample PF 0.92 → 0.63) by removing profitable counter-trend trades, and **EMA200-H1 alignment** on TECH100 removed 840 trades while pushing expectancy toward zero (+0.013R → +0.007R). Positive HTF-filter evidence exists only in daily-bar equity/ETF strategies (XLP: 316 trades, 73% WR, PF 2) and vendor material, with no unfiltered baseline.

### Cited Findings
- XAUUSD H1 breakout EA (in-sample 2023–24, OOS 2025–26): "A directional daily-EMA trend filter — 'only trade with the daily trend' — made both samples worse, taking profit factor from 0.92 to 0.63 in-sample. It removed profitable counter-trend breakouts." Rejected. In contrast a *direction-agnostic* daily ADX gate (15–22) was kept. Disabling reversal signals also made results worse. — [MQL5 blog 773874](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/773874) [measured, partial]
- US_TECH100 H1 MACD: adding "buys only above EMA200, sells only below" to the regime-filtered set: trades 1,634 → 794, WR 46.1% → 45.8%, expectancy +0.013R → +0.007R, PF 1.02 → 1.01, DD −33.4R → −29.6R; author: "the 'trade with the trend' rule did not add edge here, at least on H1 using EMA200 as the trend definition". Cumulative test, not isolated. — [MQL5 article 22290](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/22290) [measured]
- Equity/ETF daily evidence *for* HTF filters: XLP with close > close 250 days ago AND close > close 22 days ago, 3-day-low pullback entry, exit on first up close: 316 trades, 73% WR, PF 2.0, avg +0.28%/trade, max DD 10%; **no version without the filters is reported**; author's framing: multi-timeframe analysis "is not a standalone edge, but … a powerful framework for improving existing strategies". — [Quantified Strategies, multi-timeframe](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/multi-timeframe-analysis-and-strategy) [measured, no baseline]
- Reddit 15-symbol equity test: trend-regime alignment cut trades 74% and lowered avg return/trade (0.48% → 0.46%); helped on strongly trending names (AAPL risk ratio 0.358 → 0.509) and hurt or did nothing on others. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1skdizm/snapshots/2026-04-13T15%3A22%3A00.465913Z) [measured]
- Gold H1 EMA-trend-filtered EA (forum post, real ticks 99%, fixed 1 lot, spread model not stated): Jan 2024–May 2026, 1,631 trades, PF 1.34, max balance DD 32%, 22/29 months profitable; Dec 2025 win rate fell to 31% with a 14-trade losing streak; author estimates PF ≈ 1.2–1.3 excluding outlier months. No filter-off comparison. — [MQL5 forum 509738 p.2](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/509738/page2) [measured, no baseline]
- Vendor/indicator claims that M15 alignment "filters out many weak counter-trend entries" on M1 gold come without any numbers. — [TradingView Trend Stripes MTF](https://www.tradingview.com/script/A2ymKv7k-Trend-Stripes-MTF/) [vendor claim]
- A backtesting guide's framing: adding a 4H filter "mechanically reduces the number of active 15M signals"; test whether the filter reduces losses *during ranges* — "if it doesn't, it adds no real value"; beware look-ahead from HTF bar alignment (use last *closed* HTF bar). — [Backtrex MTF guide](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/multi-timeframe-backtesting-guide) [opinion/method]

### Inferences
- On gold intraday, the measured direction of effect of *directional* HTF alignment is zero-to-negative; the mechanism in both studies is that gold's counter-trend intraday moves are themselves profitable for short-horizon entries. Our "against-M15 entries did not lose more" is therefore consistent with external evidence and should not be "fixed" by adding a hard M15/H1 directional gate.
- A **direction-agnostic** HTF *strength* gate (daily/H1 ADX above a low threshold, or an HTF range-width check) has the only positive measured gold evidence and is the better candidate if any HTF filter is added.
- Where HTF filters look good (daily equities), the base strategies are long-only mean-reversion in secular uptrends with no reported baseline; this is not transferable to two-sided M1 gold scalping.

### Gaps
- No M1-entry / M15-filter study exists with numbers on gold or FX; both controlled studies are H1 entries with daily/EMA200 filters.
- No study tests HTF *slope* or HTF *EMA gap* as a filter.

## KQ6 — Volatility regime: evidence on skipping trades when M1 ATR is abnormally high (e.g., > 2× its 24h median) or right after a large daily range

### Takeaway
The measured evidence **does not support skipping high-volatility periods for trend/breakout entries**: in the TECH100 study the VOLATILE regime (ATR above the 80th percentile of 500 bars) was the *second most profitable* bucket (+0.042R, 48.5% WR) and excluding it is why the regime filter failed; a 2026 micro-study found losses concentrated in *mid*-volatility, with both low and high σ profitable. For gold specifically the only kept volatility rule is a two-sided range cap (skip days with Asian range < 0.30% or > 1.60% of price). Academic work confirms gold volatility clusters intraday and interday (so a large range predicts more range), but no study links a large daily range to next-day trend-entry expectancy.

### Cited Findings
- TECH100 H1 MACD, raw results by regime (ATR percentile of 500 bars, 20th/80th cut-offs; ADX 20/25): VOLATILE 501 trades, WR 48.5%, **+0.042R**; NORMAL 435, 47.4%, +0.036R; TREND 1,199, 45.6%, +0.005R; RANGE 78, 42.3%, −0.069R. Removing VOLATILE+RANGE (579 trades) left PF unchanged (1.03 → 1.02) and worsened DD (−28.8R → −33.4R). — [MQL5 article 22290](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/22290) [measured]
- Volatility-regime micro-study (instrument/timeframe/costs not stated; 20-period realised σ; quartile cut-offs): avg PnL per trade **low σ +26.9 to +30.2 bps, mid σ −3.0 to −6.0 bps, high σ +28.3 to +32.0 bps**; skipping mid-σ cut trades ~47% and raised avg PnL ≈ 18 → ≈ 28 bps and win rate 55% → 60%+. — [askrubyai Day 29](https://askrubyai.github.io/blog/posts/2026-03-11-volatility-regime-filter/index.html) [measured, poorly documented]
- Gold range-breakout (Jan 2023–Sep 2026 real ticks): skip when the 03:00–08:00 range is < 0.30% or > 1.60% of price; kept in the final design; "no range filter" produced false breakouts (narrow) and late moves (wide), qualitative. Final PF 1.37–1.38, DD 17–19% at 1% risk; 5R target lowered PF to 1.30. — [MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540) [measured; filter effect qualitative]
- XAUUSD H1 breakout EA: a *tighter* opening-range width cap "made results worse" (no numbers) and was shipped disabled; stops at 5–6×ATR with 0.2–0.5R targets produced 68–87% win rates but PF ≈ 1.0–1.1 (net ≈ 0). — [MQL5 blog 773874](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/773874) [measured, partial]
- Daily-equity volatility filters: a 63-day historical-vol filter (threshold 0.6 relative vol; S&P 500, RUT, NDX; frictionless) "generally better" than buy-and-hold with "pretty big drawdowns" remaining; author: not viable standalone. — [R-bloggers, HV filter](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2013/03/a-volatility-filter-using-historical-vol/) [measured, image-only]. A quantstrat MA strategy with a volatility filter saw the filter *hurt* overall return and the author concluded "the choice of volatility measure mattered a lot". — [R-bloggers, SMA + vol filter part 2](https://www.r-bloggers.com/simple-moving-average-strategy-with-a-volatility-filter-follow-up-part-2/) [measured]. A VIX split showed one intraday strategy *losing* when VIX < 20 — opposite of "skip high vol". — [QuantRocket VIX notebook](https://www.quantrocket.com/codeload/first-last/first_last/Part4-VIX-Research.ipynb.html) [measured]
- Gold volatility structure: Batten & Lucey (Applied Economics Letters 17(2), 2010) on CBOT gold futures 1999–2005 with Garman–Klass range volatility find significant intraday and day-of-week variation and clear volatility clustering; Cai, Cheung & Wong (JFM 2001, COMEX) find intraday patterns "exert a profound impact on the dynamics of return volatility". — [RePEc, Batten & Lucey](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apeclt/v17y2010i2p187-190.html); [TCD working paper](https://www.tcd.ie/triss/assets/PDFs/iiis/iiisdp225.pdf) [academic]
- Intraday caveat: "volatility follows the clock" — an ATR percentile computed over a rolling intraday window cycles with session opens/closes and should be benchmarked per hour or read from a higher timeframe. — [LuxAlgo Volatility Switch](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/volatility-switch.md) [opinion]
- A gold scalping EA forum reported the 20:00–01:00 UTC window "tends to hurt performance" with spread widening at 21:00–22:00 UTC. — [MQL5 blog 770479](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770479) [opinion]

### Inferences
- A rule like "skip when M1 ATR > 2× its 24h median" has **no supporting evidence and one contradicting data point** (VOLATILE bucket profitable). The volatility risk for a scalper is execution (spread/slippage), not directional expectancy; that argues for a *spread*-based gate and a stop/target scaled in ATR rather than an ATR-level skip.
- Because ATR on M1 is strongly time-of-day periodic on gold, "2× the 24h median" will fire almost every London/NY open and almost never in Asia; if a volatility gate is kept it should be relative to the same-hour baseline (e.g., ATR vs median ATR of that hour over the last 20 sessions).
- Two-sided *range* filters (skip both too-narrow and too-wide prior ranges) have some gold support; the "too-wide" side (e.g., prior-session range > ~1.6% of price) is the closest evidence-backed analogue to "skip after a large daily range" — but the effect was never quantified in isolation.

### Gaps
- No study quantifies next-session trend-entry expectancy after a large daily range on gold; only volatility clustering is documented.
- No test of an ATR-spike skip rule (×1.5/×2/×3 of a rolling median) on any FX/gold intraday series.
- The askrubyai study omits instrument, timeframe, period and costs; treat as an existence proof that "skip mid-vol" can beat "skip high-vol", not as a parameter.

## Cross-cutting methodological notes for the writer (sourced)
- Vendor/MQL5-market statistics (WR 80%+, PF 2.5–5.6, 2025–26 gold) come almost exclusively from in-sample windows that coincide with gold's strongest bull run; one MQL5 article itself notes "the backtest period overlaps with the development period". — [MQL5 Atlas Scalper write-up](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777377); Reddit XAUUSD 81.72% WR thread (Jan 2025–Mar 2026) was critiqued for the same reason — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1s5lmfh/snapshots/2026-04-03T17%3A02%3A31.736953Z) [opinion].
- The MT5 tester "does not accurately model" live spread during news/rollover; spread model is approximate and slippage simplified. — [MQL5 blog 770479](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770479)
- A consistent pattern across every controlled study found (Oxford Strat ×2, TECH100, Reddit 15-symbol, Apex Helios): stacked entry filters cut trade count 25–90% while per-trade expectancy stays flat or falls; the exceptions with real improvement were a **session/liquidity filter** (TECH100) and a **low-threshold direction-agnostic ADX gate** (gold H1). This is the strongest available prior for ranking which of our filters to test first.
