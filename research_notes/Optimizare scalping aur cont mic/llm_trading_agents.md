# LLM-driven discretionary trading agents (2024–2026): evidence, failure modes, gate designs, cost control

Scope: LLMs (GPT-4/4o/5, Claude, Gemini, Qwen, DeepSeek, open models) used as autonomous traders or as an entry "gate" over mechanical rules, with emphasis on short horizons (minutes to a day). Relevant to a Claude-gated XAUUSD M1 scalping bot. Each finding is tagged **[LIVE]**, **[PAPER-LIVE]** (forward paper trading), **[BACKTEST]**, **[SURVEY/POSITION]** or **[ANECDOTE]**. Research date: 2026-11-09.

Important caveat for the whole file: I found **no study at all** that tests an LLM gate on a 1-minute (or even 5/15-minute) FX/gold scalping system. The closest cadence in a live setting is Alpha Arena (decision roughly every 2–3 minutes on crypto perps / US equities), and it is a negative result. Everything else is daily or slower.

---

## Key Question 1: What live or out-of-sample evidence exists (2024–2026) that LLM agents add edge on short horizons? Results, costs, caveats.

### Takeaway
Live evidence at short horizons is uniformly negative or at best noisy: in nof1's Alpha Arena (real money, decisions every few minutes) 4 of 6 models lost money in Season 1 and only 6 of 32 model-track results were positive in the US-equity season, with fees from overtrading eating up to 13% of starting capital. Daily-cadence live benchmarks (LiveTradeBench, Agent Market Arena) show all models profitable in a rising stock market but with returns largely explained by passive exposure; leakage-controlled and long-window backtests (FINSABER, KTD-Fin) show no persistent alpha over buy-and-hold. No source documents LLM edge on minute-scale XAUUSD or FX.

### Cited Findings

**Live, real-money, minute-scale cadence (Alpha Arena, nof1)**
- **[LIVE]** Alpha Arena Season 1 (crypto perps on Hyperliquid, ~Oct 18 – Nov 3 2025, $10,000 per model, six LLMs under identical prompts): four of six finished below $10k. ChatGPT (GPT-5) lost $6,267 (~63%), Gemini 2.5 Pro lost $5,671, Grok 4 lost $4,531, Claude Sonnet 4.5 lost $3,081; DeepSeek +$489, Qwen3 Max +$2,232. — [Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/)
- **[LIVE]** Win rates for all six models were 25–30%. Gemini made 238 trades vs Claude Sonnet 38. Qwen3 Max paid $1,654 in fees, Gemini $1,331 (>13% of starting capital). nof1: "PnL was dominated by trading costs in early runs" because agents over-traded, taking small gains that fees wiped out. — [Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/); fee/trade figures also in [Buildix](https://www.buildix.trade/blog/nof1-alpha-arena-lessons-ai-trading-byok-strategy-advisor-2026)
- **[LIVE]** nof1 founder Jay Azhang: "LLMs don't really handle numerical time series data very well, but that's all the context we gave them." — [Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/)
- **[LIVE]** Alpha Arena decision cadence is roughly every 2 minutes (vs 30 min for AI Investing Arena, days for rallies.ai, weekly for Nathan Smith's account). — [Flat Circle, "AI Trading Arenas"](https://blog.flatcircle.ai/p/ai-trading-arenas)
- **[LIVE]** Alpha Arena US-equity season (eight frontier models, $10k per challenge, four two-week tracks: signal trading, "Monk Mode" defense, situational awareness/rival monitoring, max leverage): combined portfolio fell about one third; only 6 of 32 individual results ended positive. - **[LIVE]** Azhang on US-equity season: models mistimed entries and exits, sized positions poorly, and flipped positions too often; Claude mostly leaned long, Gemini was comfortable shorting, Qwen used heavy leverage. — [Briefs Finance](https://www.briefs.co/news/public-ai-trading-contests-largely-lose-money-and-raise-new/)
- **[LIVE]** Flat Circle's review of 11 public arenas: every arena had at least one model with gains, but only two had a profitable median model; "the median model loses money, except in long-only arenas during rising equity markets"; "no model consistently dominates"; newer frontier models generally beat older ones. Author's conclusion: "limited evidence of LLMs beating the market at scale or with statistical significance." — [Flat Circle](https://blog.flatcircle.ai/p/ai-trading-arenas)
- **[LIVE]** Alexander Izydorczyk (ex-Coatue, NX1 Capital): none of the AI trading bots he follows has shown a durable advantage. Jim Moran (Flat Circle): most public trials are too short and noisy to settle the question. — [Briefs Finance](https://www.briefs.co/news/public-ai-trading-contests-largely-lose-money-and-raise-new/)
- **[LIVE]** TradeRank crypto-only season (9 models, identical data/rules): only 2 of 9 positive as of May 4 2026 (MiniMax M2.7 +1.02%, DeepSeek V4 Pro +0.08%); Claude Opus 4.7 last at −3.70%. Aster "Humans vs AI" crypto futures: AIs beat the average human but 9 of the top 10 were human. — [Flat Circle](https://blog.flatcircle.ai/p/ai-trading-arenas)
- **[PAPER-LIVE]** TradeRank arena (one decision per day at 16:00 UTC; as of 2026-11-08: 78 models, 11 seasons, 3,131 trades, 38.9% of model-seasons profitable; FAQ gives 43.2% — unreconciled). Season-2 examples: "TheTradingFox" 81.3% win rate but −0.35% (avg win $2.27, avg loss $20.91); Gemini 3.0 Flash 84 trades, $77.98 fees, −3.46%. — [TradeRank prompt guide](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/ai-trading-prompts-engineering)

**Live, daily cadence (academic benchmarks)**
- **[LIVE]** LiveTradeBench (Yu, Li, You; arXiv 2511.03628): 21 LLM backbones, 50 trading days Aug 18 – Oct 24 2025, one decision per trading day, US stocks + Polymarket. US stocks: all 21 models profitable; best GPT-4.1 +6.25% (Sharpe 2.64), GPT-o3 +6.04%; worst Qwen3-235B-Thinking +1.78%. Polymarket: only 4 of 21 profitable (Claude-Sonnet-3.7 +20.54%, Grok-4 +7.38%); Qwen3-235B-Thinking −57.62% (MDD 72%), GPT-o3 −54.84%, Kimi-K2 −53.44%. Spearman correlation of LMArena score with return: 0.054 (stocks), −0.38 (Polymarket). — [arXiv 2511.03628 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1); abstract: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03628)
- **[LIVE]** LiveTradeBench: delaying decisions by k days hurt Polymarket returns by ~2% but slightly improved stock returns at k=2 — i.e., faster reaction did not help in equities. News was the most-cited factor in agent reasoning, then price history. — [arXiv 2511.03628 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)
- **[LIVE]** Agent Market Arena ("When Agents Trade", Qian et al., arXiv 2510.11695, Oct 2025): four agent frameworks (InvestorAgent, TradeAgent, HedgeFundAgent, DeepFundAgent) × backbones GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Claude-3.5-haiku, Claude-sonnet-4, Gemini-2.0-flash, live in crypto and stocks. Finding: agent framework explains more outcome variation than the LLM backbone; frameworks range from aggressive to conservative. No returns on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2510.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695)

**Backtests that overturn earlier positive claims**
- **[BACKTEST]** FINSABER (Li, Kim, Cucuringu, Ma; arXiv 2505.07078, KDD 2026 D&B oral): FinMem's reported TSLA result (Sharpe 2.679, CR 61.8%, Oct 2022–Apr 2023) fell to Sharpe 0.927/CR 19.9% when rerun with GPT-4o-mini and Sharpe 0.404/CR 5.3% with GPT-4o. — [arXiv 2505.07078 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)
- **[BACKTEST]** FINSABER extended window (2004–2024, TSLA/NFLX/AMZN/MSFT): FinMem Sharpe 0.641/0.293/0.188/0.203 vs buy-and-hold 0.630/0.622/0.551/0.461; FinAgent 0.206/−0.419/0.364/0.285. Bias-mitigated composite over historical S&P 500 constituents: FinMem Sharpe −0.253 (Random-5), 0.025 (Momentum), −0.228 (Volatility) vs buy-and-hold 0.315/0.384/0.703; paired t-test p-values vs buy-and-hold 3e-6 to 4e-5 for FinMem. FinMem alpha −1.34% (Momentum), FinAgent +6.57% but p=0.345. FinMem's commission ratio is 5–9× FinAgent's, which the authors link to overtrading. Regime Sharpe: buy-and-hold 0.61 bull / −0.28 bear; FinAgent 0.12 / −0.38; FinMem −0.19 / −0.97 — "too cautious in bulls, too aggressive in bears." — [arXiv 2505.07078 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5); abstract [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)
- **[BACKTEST]** KTD-Fin memory-controlled benchmark (Zhu et al., arXiv 2605.28359, May 2026): ten frontier LLM agents on CSI300 2024–2026 with tickers/dates anonymised; under leakage control, LLM agents' cumulative returns are "largely explained by passive market and style exposure," with limited evidence of persistent stock-selection alpha; masking substantially changes agent rationales. — [arXiv 2605.28359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28359)
- **[BACKTEST]** TradingAgents (arXiv 2412.20138): reports ≥23.21% cumulative / 24.90% annual return on three sampled stocks, beating baselines by 6.1%; authors themselves note the exceptionally high Sharpe came from a test period with few pullbacks; no live evidence. — [arXiv 2412.20138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20138)
- **[BACKTEST, single-stock negative result]** "llm-stock-predictor-postmortem" (NSE RELIANCE, local 8B LLM, 22 paper days, then a 10-year rebuild with classical ML): intraday open-to-close direction best model 53.72% accuracy = 5.47 bps/day gross edge; breakeven at 20 bps round-trip costs needs ~62% accuracy; every model turned unprofitable between 6 and 10 bps of cost; net of 20 bps the random forest returned −10.14%/yr (Sharpe −0.64, MDD −55.6%). Author: constraint is "cost and market efficiency, not information." Note: the rebuild tested ML, not an LLM directly. — [GitHub postmortem](https://github.com/AbhayPhalswal/llm-stock-predictor-postmortem)
- **[BACKTEST/SIM]** "AI Trading: Evaluating LLMs for Technical Market Analysis" (Ntale, Georgia Tech MS paper, arXiv 2607.15414): GPT-4 Turbo, Claude 3 Opus, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3 70B, FinGPT on candlestick recognition and BUY/SELL/HOLD; GPT-4 Turbo had the best annualised return/Sharpe among general models and beat passive S&P 500 "under tested conditions"; "numerical hallucination" named as a persistent failure mode across all models; inconsistent performance in sideways markets. No accuracy figures on the abstract page; single-author MS thesis, treat as weak. — [arXiv 2607.15414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15414)

**Surveys / position papers on the overall evidence**
- **[SURVEY]** "Agentic Trading: When LLM Agents Meet Financial Markets" (Xia et al., arXiv 2605.19337, May/Oct 2026): 185 studies, 80 closed-loop systems; examines backtesting splits, transaction-cost realism, survivorship handling, execution fidelity. — [arXiv 2605.19337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19337). A search-summary characterised a related audit as concluding that evidence for durable alpha remains thin and practical value lies in execution, monitoring and research support — I could not verify this wording on the abstract page, so treat it as unconfirmed.
- **[POSITION]** "The Alpha Illusion" (Ye et al., arXiv 2605.16895, May 2026): public evidence cannot separate real predictive ability from temporal contamination, unmodeled frictions, Sharpe uncertainty from short windows, narrative fitting, and parametric priors; "language confidence is not tradable probability, narrative reasoning is not numerical execution." Proposes six validity tests (temporal integrity, real-world frictions, counterfactual robustness, predictive calibration, numerical execution, multi-agent disaggregation) and recommends LLMs as auditable information interfaces upstream of independent calibration, risk and execution modules. — [arXiv 2605.16895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16895)
- **[POSITION]** "Evaluating LLMs in Finance Requires Explicit Bias Consideration" (Kong, Lopez-Lira, Zohren et al., arXiv 2602.14233, Feb 2026): reviewed 164 papers 2023–2025; five compounding biases (look-ahead, survivorship, narrative, objective, cost); no single bias discussed in more than 28% of studies. — [arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14233)
- **[ANECDOTE/COMMENTARY]** Jiri Pik (July 2026): LLM-generated backtests are "laundered alpha" via four channels (wrong code, memorized future, silent best-of-N search, crowding); cites Bridgewater's AI fund +11.9% in 2025 vs +33% for human Pure Alpha; recommends LLMs for mechanical tasks with reliable evaluators (execution, portfolio, risk), not price forecasting. — [jiripik.com](https://jiripik.com/2026/07/11/llm-designing-trading-strategies/)

**Gold/XAUUSD specifically**
- **[PAPER-LIVE]** The only XAUUSD item found: a Claude-Code-built gold bot deciding every minute on GC=F bars, paper trading, +5.56% on $10k (win rate 54.2%, PF 1.36, MDD −3.74%, 131 trades, period unstated). Decisions come from an RL model, not from Claude; Claude only runs the weekly retrain routine. NotebookLM flagged the champion's backtest (Sharpe 3.62, PF 1.24) as likely overfit. — [learnwithmeai](https://www.learnwithmeai.com/p/i-built-a-gold-predicting-trading)
- No independent, verified live P&L for any GPT-4/Claude XAUUSD scalper exists in search results; only vendor pages and GitHub feature lists. — [search summary; e.g. ai-gold-scalper repo](https://github.com/clayandthepotter/ai-gold-scalper)

### Inferences
- Where LLM agents "worked" live (LiveTradeBench stocks, rallies.ai long-only), the period was a rising equity market and all models were long-biased; this is beta, not skill — consistent with the KTD-Fin factor attribution and Flat Circle's "median loses except long-only in rising markets."
- The only live test at a cadence comparable to M1/M5 scalping (Alpha Arena, ~2-minute loop, numeric time series as the only context) is the clearest negative result in the literature: fee drag from overtrading and 25–30% win rates. For a small XAUUSD account, this is the most directly relevant data point.
- Across sources, dispersion between models given identical prompts is huge (Gemini 238 trades vs Claude 38; Qwen 1,418 vs Grok 158). Any single live run of one model for two weeks says almost nothing about expected edge.

### Gaps
- No study tests an LLM on 1-minute or 5-minute FX/gold data with realistic spread/commission; nothing on XAUUSD specifically beyond one RL paper-trade.
- Alpha Arena's own site (alphaarena.ai) returned HTTP 429, so exact prompts, cadence and per-season P&L tables could not be verified from the primary source; figures above come from Protos, Briefs, Flat Circle and Buildix.
- API cost for the live academic benchmarks (LiveTradeBench, AMA) is not reported.
- A "Bloomberg report, May 2026" on AI contests losing money and overtrading surfaced in one search summary but the Briefs article actually links to Business Standard; I could not locate the Bloomberg piece.

## Key Question 2: Which failure modes are documented for LLM trading decisions, and which prompt/architecture fixes are shown to help (with numbers)?

### Takeaway
Documented failure modes: overtrading/fee drag, regime miscalibration (timid in bulls, aggressive in bears), the "high win-rate trap" (many small wins, few large losses), run-to-run non-determinism and action flipping even at temperature 0, numerical hallucination, uniform trust in all inputs, and propagation of small state errors into runaway exposure. Fixes with any quantitative support: hard external constraints/strict mode for non-reasoning models, consensus/selective weighting across agents, trading plans with invalidation levels fed back into later calls, LLM as semantic filter over a statistical screen (win rate 51.4%→54.5%, average loss $649→$347), and server-side validation of every output. Most "prompt tricks" (no-trade default, numbered cascades, counter-case) are practitioner heuristics without controlled tests.

### Cited Findings

**Failure modes**
- **Overtrading / fee drag [LIVE]:** see Q1 — Gemini 238 trades, $1,331 fees (>13% of capital); Qwen 1,418 trades in one two-week track; nof1: PnL dominated by trading costs. — [Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/); [Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/public-ai-trading-contests-largely-lose-money-and-raise-new/)
- **Overtrading [BACKTEST]:** FinMem's commission ratio 5–9× FinAgent's; FinMem average max-drawdown duration 210 days vs FinAgent 150 (Momentum universe). — [arXiv 2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)
- **Regime miscalibration [BACKTEST]:** LLM strategies overly conservative in bull markets (underperform passive) and overly aggressive in bear markets (heavy losses); authors recommend "trend detection and regime-aware risk controls" over more framework complexity. — [arXiv 2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)
- **High win-rate trap [PAPER-LIVE, 2025 live-forward]:** Strat-LLM (Huang & Yu, IJCNN 2026, arXiv 2605.06024): standard LLMs "optimize for small, frequent gains at the expense of total returns," mitigated only by deep reasoning or strict external guardrails. — [arXiv 2605.06024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06024). Same pattern live: TradeRank "TheTradingFox" 81.3% win rate, −0.35% P&L (avg win $2.27, avg loss $20.91). — [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/ai-trading-prompts-engineering)
- **Instruction non-compliance [PAPER-LIVE]:** a "long only" prompt still produced 2 shorts among 17 opening actions; the author's rule: natural-language rules are "suggestions, not laws," so limits must be enforced server-side. — [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/ai-trading-prompts-engineering)
- **Run-to-run inconsistency / action flipping [BACKTEST]:** AlphaForgeBench (Zhang et al., arXiv 2602.18481, Feb/May 2026): LLMs show "extreme run-to-run variance," "inconsistent action sequences even under deterministic decoding," and "irrational action flipping across adjacent time steps"; attributed to stateless autoregression and continuous-to-discrete action mapping; recommends LLMs generate alpha factors/strategies rather than discrete trade actions. — [arXiv 2602.18481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18481). Search summary adds Gemini 3 Pro least consistent, Claude Sonnet 4.5 little temperature effect but instability persists — not verified on abstract page.
- **Non-determinism at temperature 0 [GENERAL]:** "Non-Determinism of 'Deterministic' LLM Settings" (arXiv 2408.04667) found accuracy variation up to 15% across runs at temperature 0. — [arXiv 2408.04667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04667)
- **Divergent decisions under identical conditions [BACKTEST]:** TrustTrade (Li et al., arXiv 2603.22567, Mar 2026): LLMs exhibit a "uniform trust" bias, treating heterogeneous retrieved sources as equally factual, making agents vulnerable to multi-source noise and "hallucination-driven volatility." — [arXiv 2603.22567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22567)
- **Numerical hallucination [BACKTEST]:** named as persistent across GPT-4 Turbo, Claude 3 Opus, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3 70B, FinGPT. — [arXiv 2607.15414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15414). Practitioner: LLMs are "language models, not calculators"; never let the model invent prices or compute indicators from memory. — [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/ai-trading-prompts-engineering); nof1: LLMs handle numeric time series poorly. — [Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/)
- **State-corruption cascade [BACKTEST]:** TradeTrap (Yan et al., arXiv 2512.02261, Dec 2025): small perturbations to a single component (market intelligence, strategy, ledger, execution) propagate through the decision loop and produce "extreme concentration, runaway exposure, and large portfolio drawdowns" in both adaptive and procedural agents. — [arXiv 2512.02261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02261)
- **Model "personalities" / persistent biases [LIVE]:** Claude Sonnet 4.5 ran a 100% long book with no hedging or stop-loss discipline; Grok 4 traded X sentiment and "bought tops and sold bottoms"; GPT-5 showed overconfident sizing and slow adaptation to regime shifts; all models showed "consistent biases." — [Buildix](https://www.buildix.trade/blog/nof1-alpha-arena-lessons-ai-trading-byok-strategy-advisor-2026); [Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/)
- **Slow information aggregation [BENCHMARK]:** Prophet Arena (Yang et al., arXiv 2510.17638, ICLR 2026): bottlenecks are inaccurate event recall, misunderstanding data sources, and slower information aggregation than markets as resolution nears. — [arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17638)
- **Silent infrastructure bugs [ANECDOTE]:** postmortem's live dashboard showed `random.gauss` values as "live" numbers and a news fetcher silently failed over to a neutral placeholder after a schema change. — [GitHub postmortem](https://github.com/AbhayPhalswal/llm-stock-predictor-postmortem)

**Fixes with quantitative or at least documented support**
- **LLM as semantic filter/veto over a mechanical screen [BACKTEST, rolling]:** "LLM as a Risk Manager" (Kim et al., Lopez-Lira, Lee, Choi; arXiv 2602.07048, Feb 2026): Granger-causality lead-lag screen on Kalshi Economics markets, then LLM re-ranks candidates on economic plausibility. Win rate 51.4% → 54.5%; average losing trade $649 → $347; 18 rolling evaluations; at a 7-day holding horizon total PnL more than doubles, "driven mainly by a pronounced reduction in downside risk." Authors: LLM's contribution is filtering out statistically fragile links, not finding rare big winners. — [arXiv 2602.07048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07048); win-rate/horizon details in [search summary of v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07048v2)
- **Strict external constraints for standard models [PAPER-LIVE 2025]:** Strat-LLM's Free / Guided / Strict modes: reasoning-heavy models peak in Free mode, standard models need Strict mode ("vital risk anchor"); Strict limits drawdowns in downtrends; a 35B model performs best under strict constraints, a 122B model pays an "alignment tax" under rigid rules but gains in Guided mode. No numeric tables on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2605.06024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06024)
- **Selective consensus across agents [BACKTEST]:** TrustTrade weights each agent's signal by semantic and numerical agreement with others, discounts divergent/weakly grounded/temporally inconsistent inputs, anchors on deterministic temporal signals, and adds reflective memory for test-time risk adjustment; in high-noise windows (2024 Q1, 2026 Q1) this moves behaviour to a "mid-risk, mid-return profile." No numbers on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2603.22567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22567)
- **Trading plan + invalidation fed back [LIVE, qualitative]:** nof1 had models write a trading plan (price target, stop loss, invalidation conditions) and the narrative behind each trade, and fed it back into later calls to combat non-determinism. — [Flat Circle](https://blog.flatcircle.ai/p/ai-trading-arenas). TradeRank requires an invalidation price on the losing side for every open/add, enforced by a 15-minute monitor. — [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/ai-trading-prompts-engineering)
- **Reminding the model of its bias [ANECDOTE]:** Doug Clinton (Intelligent Alpha) says models have distinct personalities and that reminding a model about its bias can improve results; Intelligent Alpha's tighter brief (filings, forecasts, transcripts, macro, ≤10 web searches) scores better. — [Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/public-ai-trading-contests-largely-lose-money-and-raise-new/)
- **Structured, validated output; hard limits outside the model [PRACTITIONER]:** TradeRank arena: JSON-only decisions through server-side validation (minimum confidence 0.80 for opens/adds, max 1 new position per cycle, max 10 open, cash check); numbered top-down timeframe cascade; explicit "Doing nothing is valid" in the system prompt; default no-trade when weekly/daily conflict; pre-compute all indicators and verify any position size the model returns; ask the model to argue the counter-case. Author explicitly states these templates were not backtested as a controlled treatment and that prompts "do not make a model predictive." — [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/ai-trading-prompts-engineering)
- **"LLM proposes, risk layer disposes" [OPEN-SOURCE DESIGN]:** LLM-Auto-Trader pipeline: Data → ContextBuilder → LLM → JSON validation → deterministic risk layer → execution. — [GitHub LLM-Auto-Trader](https://github.com/Erfaniaa/LLM-Auto-Trader/). Same principle from Jiri Pik: "the model proposes; the desk disposes." — [jiripik.com](https://jiripik.com/2026/07/11/llm-designing-trading-strategies/)
- **Modular design recommendation [POSITION]:** The Alpha Illusion: LLMs as auditable information interfaces upstream of independent calibration, risk and execution modules. — [arXiv 2605.16895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16895)
- **Factor generation instead of trade actions [BACKTEST]:** AlphaForgeBench: having LLMs generate alpha factors/strategies rather than discrete actions "eliminates execution-induced instability" (evidence not shown on abstract page). — [arXiv 2602.18481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18481)
- **Screening funnel before the LLM [LIVE, qualitative]:** rallies.ai narrows a large universe with technicals/options flow/fundamentals/catalysts before the LLM decides; author believes results are mostly factor-driven. — [Flat Circle](https://blog.flatcircle.ai/p/ai-trading-arenas)

### Inferences
- The one setup with a measured positive effect — LLM as a *veto* over a statistical signal, improving win rate and halving average loss rather than adding winners — is exactly the "gate over mechanical rules" architecture. The mechanism was semantic plausibility of an economic link, i.e. the LLM had text to reason over. In a pure-numeric M1 gold setup the LLM has no comparable semantic input, so the transfer is uncertain.
- Every source that measures trade counts shows the LLM's trade frequency is an uncontrolled, model-dependent variable (38 vs 238 vs 1,418 trades under identical prompts). A gate therefore needs a *hard* per-session/per-hour cap outside the model, not a prompt instruction.
- Because action flipping across adjacent steps is documented even at temperature 0, a gate that is re-polled every minute will produce contradictory verdicts; requiring agreement across N consecutive calls (or N sampled calls) before acting is the direct analogue of TrustTrade's consensus and nof1's plan feedback.

### Gaps
- No controlled A/B of "numbered checklist" or "explicit no-trade default" prompts against a baseline prompt in a trading backtest; all such advice is practitioner heuristics.
- Strat-LLM and TrustTrade numeric results were not retrievable from abstract pages.
- No paper quantifies how often an LLM hallucinates a price level/indicator value when given OHLCV text; "numerical hallucination" is named but not measured in what I could access.
- No evidence on "forcing the model to cite the numeric inputs" as a calibration or compliance fix beyond TradeRank's recommendation to cite supplied filings.

## Key Question 3: Are there studies on LLM confidence calibration in trading decisions — does a stated "confidence 0.8" mean anything?

### Takeaway
A stated confidence is at best an *ordinal* ranking signal, not a probability: the strongest paper (Ordinal Gates, Cardinal Bets) shows gates based on coverage-matched ranks are invariant to recalibration, while position sizing on raw magnitudes breaks when the confidence map changes (CER loss in 8 of 9 models; joint fitting recovers +9.2 pp/yr). Position papers state flatly that "language confidence is not tradable probability," and an SSRN ablation (not independently verified) found chain-of-thought inflated stated probabilities without improving returns or calibration error.

### Cited Findings
- **[BACKTEST, OOS]** "Ordinal Gates, Cardinal Bets" (Singh & Rezaeimanesh, arXiv 2609.00187, Aug/Sep 2026, ICAIF '26): nine open-weight LLMs scoring FactSet news on Nasdaq-100; maps/scales fit on 2021, evaluated 2022–2023. A gate using coverage-matched ranks is unaffected by monotone recalibration; sizing uses score magnitude, so changing the confidence map invalidates a scale fitted to the old distribution. Transferring a scale to a mismatched map reduced certainty-equivalent return in 8 of 9 models; matching map and scale improved ensemble CER by +9.2 pp/yr (p<0.001), +5.5 pp/yr excluding the largest contributor, but only +1.6 pp/yr under an adaptive-volatility controller. Recommendation: "Confidence transformations should therefore be evaluated jointly with the downstream controllers that consume them." — [arXiv 2609.00187](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00187)
- **[POSITION]** "Language confidence is not tradable probability"; predictive calibration (e.g., ECE) is one of six validity tests required before any deployment claim; uncalibrated confidence driving sizing is a named structural risk. — [The Alpha Illusion, arXiv 2605.16895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16895)
- **[BACKTEST, unverified]** Sitjar, "Reasoning Into Confidence: A Controlled Single-Trader Ablation of In-Decision Chain-of-Thought on LLM Trading Performance and Calibration" (SSRN 6946379, ~June 2026): in a single-trader backtest across six windows, step-by-step reasoning measurably raised the model's stated probabilities without matching gains in returns or calibration error; author concludes a post-CoT stated probability should not be treated as a risk signal. SSRN page returned 403 and the paper did not appear in a follow-up search; details come only from a search-engine summary. — [SSRN 6946379](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6946379)
- **[BENCHMARK]** Prophet Arena (arXiv 2510.17638, ICLR 2026): many LLMs show "small calibration errors" and "consistent prediction confidence" on prediction-market events, with "promising market returns," but bottlenecks include inaccurate event recall and slower information aggregation than markets near resolution. — [arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17638). eWeek's write-up: GPT-5 led on accuracy but o3-mini made more money — accuracy/calibration and profit diverged. — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/can-ai-predict-future/)
- **[GENERAL, non-finance]** "Reasoning Models Better Express Their Confidence": reasoning models strictly better calibrated in 33 of 36 settings. — [via themoonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/reasoning-models-better-express-their-confidence). Counter-evidence: "Don't Think Twice! Over-Reasoning Impairs Confidence Calibration" (arXiv 2508.15050): longer chains of thought can introduce spurious rationales that hurt accuracy and calibration. — [arXiv 2508.15050](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15050)
- **[BACKTEST]** Financial vision-language model study (arXiv 2608.06532): trained probes improved calibration more than ranking; self-reported confidence often failed on the models' own mistakes. — [arXiv 2608.06532](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06532)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** TradeRank requires confidence ≥0.80 for opens/adds and enforces it server-side; no evidence is offered that 0.80 corresponds to any realised hit rate. — [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/ai-trading-prompts-engineering)

### Inferences
- For a Claude entry gate, using confidence as a *threshold/rank* ("only the top-X% most confident setups") is defensible; using it to *scale lot size* is not, unless a monotone map is fitted on the bot's own logged outcomes and refit whenever the prompt or model changes.
- A model's stated 0.8 on a 1-minute gold setup has never been measured against realised outcomes in any source found; the bot's own JSONL journal is the only way to calibrate it (isotonic/Platt fit on logged confidence vs trade outcome).

### Gaps
- No calibration study at intraday horizons or on FX/commodities; all calibration work is on news-driven equity or prediction-market events.
- Could not access full text of Ordinal Gates (per-model ECE values) or the Sitjar paper.

## Key Question 4: What designs keep API cost proportional to account size (call frequency, model routing, deciding only on M5/M15 closes)?

### Takeaway
Practitioner designs converge on: call the LLM only on a cadence tied to the strategy (daily, hourly, or when a cheap rule fires), cache the static system prompt, disable or cap hidden reasoning tokens, route routine checks to a cheap model (Flash/Haiku-class, ~30–50× cheaper) and reserve the expensive model for flagged events, and enforce daily/monthly dollar caps. The reference open-source bot shows that a 96-calls/day Opus loop with extended thinking costs ~$40–50/day, which is a structural loss on a $1,000 account with a +0.5%/day edge.

### Cited Findings
- **[OPEN-SOURCE DESIGN]** LLM-Auto-Trader per-call token budget on Opus 4.7 with extended thinking: system prompt ~1,500 tokens (cached after first call, ~10× cheaper), user context 3,000–4,000 (never cached), visible JSON output 300–800, hidden reasoning up to 8,000 billed at output rate. At 96 calls/day (4/hour) with $15/M input, $1.50/M cached input, $75/M output: ~$40–50/day, ~$1,200–1,500/month. — [GitHub LLM-Auto-Trader](https://github.com/Erfaniaa/LLM-Auto-Trader/)
- **[OPEN-SOURCE DESIGN]** Cost controls in the same bot: `LLM_DAILY_BUDGET_USD` (default $5), `LLM_MONTHLY_BUDGET_USD` (default $100) stop calls and send a Telegram report; `LLM_EXTENDED_THINKING=false` removes ~5,000 hidden tokens per call; `LLM_THINKING_BUDGET` caps it; Gemini 2.5 Flash "roughly 30–50× cheaper"; cadence `daily`/`hourly`/`adaptive` (LLM picks its own next check, bounded 15–60 min) — adaptive can spike costs if the LLM keeps asking for short intervals. Explicit warning: with $1,000 capital and a +0.5%/day edge ($5/day), a $40/day LLM bill is a structural loss; the bot "only makes economic sense" once capital is large enough; paper P&L is "an upper bound, not a forecast"; calibrate with ~$50 live. — [GitHub LLM-Auto-Trader](https://github.com/Erfaniaa/LLM-Auto-Trader/)
- **[LIVE]** Fee drag from LLM trade frequency at retail size: Gemini's $1,331 in exchange fees on $10k (>13%) in two weeks. — [Buildix](https://www.buildix.trade/blog/nof1-alpha-arena-lessons-ai-trading-byok-strategy-advisor-2026); Buildix's lesson: "trade frequency drives fee drag, which matters most at retail size" (and its extrapolation to a $50k account is unsupported). — same source
- **[VENDOR GUIDES]** Model routing typically saves 40–70% assuming ~70% of requests go to a cheaper tier; route simple queries to Haiku/Llama-8B-class models and reserve GPT-4o-class for complex events; call the API only when a cheap rule or local model flags a high-value event and compare the expected P&L gain of a call against its cost; example: 500 signals/day with 4K-token prompts on GPT-4o ≈ $60/day; routing gateways add ~5% markup (OpenRouter) or more. These are vendor blogs, unverified. — [morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/llm-cost-optimization); [predictengine](https://www.predictengine.ai/blog/llm-powered-trade-signals-via-api-5-approaches-compared); [layer3labs calculator](https://www.layer3labs.io/tools/llm-routing-cost-calculator)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** TradeRank arena: one LLM decision per day (16:00 UTC), with a cheap deterministic monitor checking invalidation levels every 15 minutes — the LLM is never in the fast loop. — [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/ai-trading-prompts-engineering)
- **[LIVE]** Flat Circle: cadences in public arenas range from ~2 min (Alpha Arena) to weekly; author argues many smaller decisions beat one large weekly allocation call because tokens get spread too thin in the latter, while listing tool access vs context size and breadth vs depth as open trade-offs. — [Flat Circle](https://blog.flatcircle.ai/p/ai-trading-arenas)
- **[LIVE]** LiveTradeBench: faster reaction (k=0 vs k=2 day delay) did not help in US stocks (returns slightly improved at k=2), only in Polymarket (~2% worse per added day). — [arXiv 2511.03628 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)
- **[COMMENTARY]** Cost of a strategy-generation query fell from ~$1 to ~$0.02 (2026). — [jiripik.com](https://jiripik.com/2026/07/11/llm-designing-trading-strategies/)
- **[ANECDOTE, user's own project memory]** The bot in this repo already applies two of these patterns: Sonnet when flat, 1-minute calls kept, with a rule that API cost must stay below trading profit. — (internal memory note `claude-every-minute-choice.md`; no external source)

### Inferences
- With the token profile above (≈5k input + up to 9k output per call), a 1-minute Claude loop is ~1,440 calls/day; even on a Haiku/Sonnet tier with caching this is one to two orders of magnitude more calls than any documented design uses, and the only live system at that cadence (Alpha Arena) lost on fees. Gating the expensive call on a mechanical pre-filter (e.g., only when the basket rules already fire, only on M5/M15 bar close) is the design every practitioner source converges on.
- The "LLM proposes, deterministic layer disposes" pattern lets the fast loop (stop/invalidation monitoring, trailing) run at zero API cost; the LLM is only consulted at decision points.

### Gaps
- No source gives an empirical curve of P&L vs call frequency for the same strategy; the LiveTradeBench delay experiment is the closest (daily granularity).
- No published figures for Anthropic prompt-cache hit rates in a trading loop; the ~10× cached-input discount is from the bot README's assumptions, not measured.

## Key Question 5: Evidence comparing "LLM as gate over a mechanical system" vs "mechanical system alone"

### Takeaway
Only one study isolates this comparison: a Granger-causality lead-lag strategy on Kalshi markets with and without an LLM plausibility filter, where the filter raised win rate from 51.4% to 54.5%, cut the average losing trade from $649 to $347, and more than doubled 7-day-horizon PnL by reducing downside — the gain was from *vetoing fragile signals*, not finding new ones. Analogous non-LLM meta-labeling (LSTM filter over trend-following futures) exists, and an LLM trading-rule framework (Strat-LLM) found strict external rules help standard models but tax large reasoning models. Everything else is "LLM vs rules" head-to-head, where attribution is confounded by system design.

### Cited Findings
- **[BACKTEST, rolling OOS]** "LLM as a Risk Manager" (arXiv 2602.07048): statistical baseline win rate 51.4% → hybrid 54.5%; average losing trade $649 → $347; gains "stable across trading configurations"; 18 rolling evaluations; at 7-day holding horizon total PnL more than doubles via downside reduction; conclusion: LLMs work as "semantic risk managers on top of statistical discovery," filtering statistically fragile links that cause large losses. The LLM used and drawdown figures were not on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2602.07048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07048); [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07048v2)
- **[BACKTEST]** Non-LLM precedent for the same architecture: a 2025 Expert Systems with Applications study used an LSTM to filter entry signals of a trend-following strategy on four index futures; the WiseTrader tutorial describes the same primary-rules-plus-secondary-filter design with filter on/off backtests. — [search summary]; [WiseTrader tutorial](https://wisetradertoolbox.com/docs/toolbox/pages/nn-tutorial-4.html)
- **[BACKTEST]** A financial-forecasting paper found its filtered and unfiltered LLM strategies had highly correlated returns and that the filter "does not translate into portfolio performance" — a case where a filter changed little. — [MemGuard-Alpha, arXiv 2603.26797](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26797) (per search summary; not read in full)
- **[PAPER-LIVE 2025]** Strat-LLM: Strict mode (external strategy rules constraining the LLM) is a "vital risk anchor" for standard models and limits drawdowns in downtrends; Free/Guided modes capture momentum in uptrends; a 122B model suffers an "alignment tax" under rigid rules. This is the inverse direction (rules gating the LLM), but shows the interaction is model-dependent. — [arXiv 2605.06024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06024)
- **[BACKTEST]** FINSABER: simple SMA crossover also failed to beat buy-and-hold (Sharpe −0.298 Random-5, −0.251 Momentum), while FinMem/FinAgent were worse still on Sharpe in most universes — i.e., neither the LLM nor the simple rule beat passive over 20 years / 100+ symbols. — [arXiv 2505.07078 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)
- **[BACKTEST]** 3S-Trader claims it "significantly outperforms traditional rule-based strategies across all sectors," attributing part of the gap to rule baselines being fixed-factor with low trading frequency — i.e., not a like-for-like comparison. — [arXiv 2510.17393](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17393)
- **[BACKTEST]** Commodity-ETF multi-agent paper: if an LLM strategy beats a rule-based benchmark it is "difficult to determine whether the improvement comes from the language model itself or from the broader system design." — [arXiv 2606.08283](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08283)
- **[BACKTEST]** Sentiment-weighted portfolios had lower max drawdowns but lower annual returns and Sharpe than purely technical strategies. — [arXiv 2510.10526](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10526v1)
- **[LIVE, qualitative]** rallies.ai: extensive mechanical screening funnel before the LLM; Flat Circle author believes results are mostly factor-driven and "too early to conclude." — [Flat Circle](https://blog.flatcircle.ai/p/ai-trading-arenas)
- **[COMMENTARY]** Jiri Pik: use LLMs for mechanical subproblems with reliable evaluators (execution, portfolio construction, risk), citing MadEvolve's OOS Sharpe +0.62 to +1.83 on such subproblems while forecasting overfit. — [jiripik.com](https://jiripik.com/2026/07/11/llm-designing-trading-strategies/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Buildix: LLMs should serve as research/interpretation tools and "structured second opinions," not autonomous execution. — [Buildix](https://www.buildix.trade/blog/nof1-alpha-arena-lessons-ai-trading-byok-strategy-advisor-2026)

### Inferences
- The positive gate result depends on the LLM having *semantic* information the statistical screen lacks (event descriptions). On M1 XAUUSD with only numeric features, a Claude gate has no information advantage over the rules it is gating; its plausible contributions are (a) vetoing trades into news/session-transition conditions it is told about, (b) enforcing checklist consistency, and (c) refusing low-quality setups — all of which could also be coded deterministically. The honest test is an A/B against the mechanical system alone with the gate's veto logged but not executed (shadow mode), which no published source has done for intraday FX/metals.
- A measurable success criterion from the one positive study: the gate should *reduce average loss size and raise win rate* on the subset it blocks, not add new winners. If blocked trades in shadow logs are not net-negative, the gate is pure cost.

### Gaps
- No study compares an LLM gate vs rules-alone on intraday FX, gold, or any sub-daily timeframe.
- No ablation separates the effect of the LLM veto from the effect of simply trading less often (a random 30% veto might produce similar fee savings) — the risk-manager paper does not report a random-filter baseline on its abstract page.
- Full text of arXiv 2602.07048 (which LLM, drawdowns, trade counts) was not retrieved.
