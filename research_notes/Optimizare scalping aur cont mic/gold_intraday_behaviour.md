# Empirical intraday behaviour of gold (XAUUSD / GC) at 1–30 minute horizons

Scope note for the report writer: this file covers only *how gold moves intraday* (momentum vs mean reversion, hourly volatility profile, impulse bars, NFP/FOMC/Friday effects, pullback failure). It does not cover execution costs, broker spreads policy or position sizing beyond what the volatility numbers imply. Research date: 2026-11-09. Roughly 20 web searches/fetches plus local text extraction of two PDFs.

Overall state of the evidence (read this first):
- Peer-reviewed work on gold *at 1–5 minute horizons* is thin and almost entirely about (a) reactions to scheduled macro news (FOMC, NFP) and (b) behaviour around the London fixings. There is **no** peer-reviewed study I could find that measures continuation-vs-reversal after large M1 bars in gold, or the failure rate of pullback-to-EMA entries. Those two questions are answered below only with adjacent evidence and must be measured on the bot's own M1 data.
- Hour-of-day volatility numbers exist but only from broker/vendor blogs with short samples; two sources disagree by a factor of 3–4 on the Asian session, mainly because gold's dollar volatility tripled between 2015–2024 and 2025–26. All USD figures below are regime-dependent and should be re-derived locally.
- Intraday *time-series momentum* (first half-hour return predicts last half-hour return) is well documented for equity-index ETFs and Chinese commodity futures (incl. Shanghai gold) at 30-minute granularity, not at 1–5 minutes, and not for COMEX/XAUUSD specifically.

## Key question 1: Is there published evidence of intraday momentum or mean reversion in gold at 1–5 minute horizons, and in which hours each dominates?

### Takeaway
No published study measures 1–5 minute momentum/mean-reversion in COMEX gold or XAUUSD by hour. The closest rigorous evidence is (i) 30-minute "intraday time-series momentum" in Shanghai gold/silver futures and other commodity futures, (ii) a daily-OHLC opening-range-breakout backtest on COMEX gold 2009–2014 that found positive, significant breakout (momentum) returns, and (iii) a daily-timeframe RSI mean-reversion test on XAUUSD that lost money. Practitioner sources consistently claim the 12:30–16:00 UTC window is the most reliable for breakouts and that Asian-session breakouts mostly fail, but these claims are not accompanied by reproducible code.

### Cited Findings
- Ma, Bouri, Xu & Zhou (2025, *Global Finance Journal*), Chinese gold and silver futures: "both intraday momentum and reversal effects are significant"; before night trading was introduced the first half-hour of daytime returns predicted later returns, after night trading "the first half-hour night returns become a significant predictor"; intraday reversals are "mainly driven by liquidity oversupply from irrational, uninformed traders"; a market-timing strategy beats always-long. Data period and effect sizes are behind the paywall (abstract only). — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/glofin/v64y2025ics1044028325000110.html); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1044028325000110)
- Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (2018, *JFE*) "Market intraday momentum": first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return in SPY (1993–2013); the effect is stronger on high-volatility, high-volume and macro-news days. This is the template paper; it is not about gold. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18301351)
- Extension to Chinese commodity futures (copper, steel, soybean, soybean meal): first half-hour return positively predicts last half-hour return across all four contracts. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531919311328)
- International evidence: intraday time-series momentum found in 12 of 16 markets tested (working paper, Lancaster 2020). — [Lancaster PDF](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2020/files/2020/04/FoFI-2020-092-Zeming-Li.pdf)
- Counter-evidence: a 2024 paper finds significant intraday *reversal* effects in Chinese commodity futures and options. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X24002865)
- Sönnert (2015, Umeå bachelor thesis) "Intraday Momentum: Day trading on the gold futures market using ORB and GARCH". Data: NYMEX/COMEX gold futures daily OHLC, 2009-05-15 to 2014-11-11, 1,384–1,386 trading days. Rule: symmetric ±10 bp thresholds from the open, one long and one short max per day, exit at close; if both thresholds break the day loses the 20 bp range. Results: long-only days 155 (avg +0.86%/day), short-only days 146 (avg +0.96%/day), both-sides "whipsaw" days 1,083 (78.25% of days, avg −0.20%, max loss −0.21%); total +56.57%; daily mean 0.04% vs 0.02% buy-and-hold; daily std 0.58% vs 1.14%; Sharpe 1.28 vs 0.34; returns significant at 99%. Conclusion: "gold futures are affected by momentum and can deviate from a random walk"; GARCH-scaled thresholds did not improve results. Caveats: student thesis, daily OHLC only (so no intrabar path, assumes fills exactly at threshold), no transaction costs reported in the extracted text, 2009–2014 regime. — [DiVA PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:845497/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- Mean reversion on XAUUSD fails on daily bars: Quant-Signals reports RSI mean reversion on XAUUSD D1 at −0.304R expectancy and concludes trend-following beats mean reversion on gold (8,693 trades across three strategies; vendor backtest, not peer-reviewed). — [Quant-Signals](https://quant-signals.com/xauusd-trading-strategies/)
- A TradingView open-source M1 RSI mean-reversion scalper for gold uses TP 10 points / SL 5 points and recommends 12–36 months of M1 data (800–2000+ trades) before trusting results; its positive-expectancy claim is unverified. — [TradingView script](https://kr.tradingview.com/script/braXKo74-Gold-Spread-Algo)
- FXNX (12 months of tick data aggregated hourly, period not dated): "Asian breakout strategies fail over 74% of the time"; after 17:00 UTC trend continuations produce "slow chops"; the 12:30–16:00 UTC window is "the most reliable for breakouts". Method not independently checked. — [FXNX hourly ATR](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/xauusd-hourly-atr-table-12-months-tick-data)
- FortTraders (opinion piece): XAUUSD "tends to trend hard around macro catalysts (Fed decisions, NFP) but chops heavily between them", hence a regime filter is needed. Folklore-level, consistent with the academic news-day findings in Q4. — [FortTraders](https://fortraders.com/blog/momentum-vs-mean-reversion-strategies-for-challenges)
- Price discovery location (relevant to which hours carry information): Hauptfleisch, Putniņš & Lucey (2016, *J. Futures Markets* 36(6):564–586), 17 years of intraday data: both London spot and NY futures contribute, but "New York futures play a larger role on average" despite NY volume being under a tenth of London's. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jfutmk:v:36:y:2016:i:6:p:564-586). Earlier daily-data study (Lucey, Larkin & O'Connor): London average information share 55%, dominance switches over time with no clear macro link; London had 86% of 2011 volume. — [White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/113760/1/2.%20London%20or%20New%20York%20%20-%20where%20and%20when%20does%20the%20gold%20price%20originate.pdf)
- Intraday *return* seasonality (not volatility): a CBS 2019 master's thesis (135 pp.) reports a "hat-shaped intraday seasonality, with gold appreciating during eastern trading hours" and depreciating the rest of the day, with drops around London fixing times in the alleged-manipulation years. Search-snippet version of the same thesis quotes average returns of −3.7% and −3.9% (units unclear, presumably basis points or annualised) in the half hour before the AM and PM fixings. Student work; PDF returned 403 so figures unverified. — [CBS portal](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/gold-price-dynamics-around-the-clock/)
- London PM fix: Abrantes-Metz & Metz screened 2001–2013 intraday data; anomalous activity around the 15:00 London (PM) fix from 2004 onward, none at the AM fix; large fix-window moves were downward at least two-thirds of the time in six separate years, and "In 2010, large moves during the fix were negative 92 percent of the time". Pre-2015 fixing mechanism, so partly historical. — [LBMA Alchemist 73](https://www.lbma.org.uk/alchemist/issue-73/has-there-been-a-decade-of-london-pm-gold-fixing-manipulation)
- Caminschi & Heaney (2014, *J. Futures Markets*), CME gold futures and GLD: significantly elevated volume and volatility immediately after the PM fixing *starts*, no significant return after the fix result is published; sample mixes pre- and post-procedure-change dates (per a Missouri State review I did not open in full). — [BearWorks review](https://bearworks.missouristate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1565&context=articles-cob)

### Inferences
- The only gold-specific momentum evidence (Sönnert ORB) says: a tiny 10 bp opening range (≈ $4 at $4,000 gold) breaks on *both* sides 78% of days — i.e. intraday whipsaw is the base case — and the strategy only wins because the ~22% of true one-directional days run far (≈ +0.9% ≈ $36 at today's prices). For an M1 scalper this translates to: expect most breakouts to fail, and profitability depends on letting the minority of real trend days run, not on win rate. The user's existing memory note that "trail captures little of peak" is consistent with this distribution.
- Momentum evidence clusters around information arrival: US-session hours (12:30–16:00 UTC), scheduled macro releases, and the first half-hour after a session open. Mean reversion / chop is the documented base state when no new information is arriving (Asian mid-session, post-17:00 UTC). This is the hour-dependence the question asks for, but it is assembled from sources at 30-min and daily granularity plus vendor claims — not from a 1–5 minute study.
- Hauptfleisch et al. implies that NY futures hours (≈ 13:20–18:30 UTC pit, electronic nearly 23h) carry more price-discovery weight; a scalper fading moves during London/NY overlap is fading informed flow, whereas fading moves in Asia is fading noise. Treat as a hypothesis to test, not a result.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures autocorrelation / variance-ratio / momentum-vs-reversion of XAUUSD or GC returns at 1, 5, 15 or 30-minute horizons split by UTC hour. This is the single most important gap and should be measured locally (variance ratio VR(k) per hour bucket on the bot's M1 history).
- Ma et al. (2025) full text (effect sizes, data period) is paywalled; Caminschi & Heaney (2014) full text was not opened.
- No academic work was found on COMEX gold "first half-hour predicts last half-hour" specifically; the commodity-futures results are for Chinese contracts.

## Key question 2: Typical 1-minute range / ATR of gold by UTC hour; London/NY overlap vs Asia ratios

### Takeaway
Two vendor datasets agree on the *shape* (trough 20:00–22:00 UTC and mid-Asia 03:00–05:00; secondary bump at 00:00–02:00 UTC Shanghai/Tokyo open; peak 12:00–15:00 UTC around US data and the NY open; London 07:00–12:00 roughly equal to or below Asia) but disagree on levels by a factor of ~3 because of sample period. Overlap-to-Asia ratio is ~1.5–2.3× on mean hourly range in 2025–26 data and ~4–5× on median hourly ATR in an earlier 12-month tick sample. No source publishes a true 1-minute ATR table; the per-minute figures below are derived.

### Cited Findings
- GoldHunts hourly study: 2,599 XAUUSD H1 bars (Twelve Data), 108–109 bars per UTC hour, "a few months" of recent data (undated, but daily figures show the sample is 2025–26 at high gold prices); range = high − low per bar. Average range by UTC hour (USD): 00h 17.31, 01h 20.59, 02h 14.93, 03h 11.30, 04h 10.29, 05h 15.28, 06h 14.12, 07h 12.36, 08h 13.55, 09h 11.44, 10h 11.86, 11h 14.14, **12h 21.91, 13h 24.09, 14h 21.61, 15h 17.16**, 16h 13.28, 17h 12.99, 18h 13.02, 19h 12.49, 20h 8.69, **21h 2.80** (rollover), 22h 15.34, 23h 12.07. Medians are close to means except 22h (median 10.55) and 18h (median 10.87). Max single-hour ranges: 13h $103, 18h $111, 22h $104. Peak/trough = 13h vs 21h ≈ 8.6×. Session share of daily range: Asia 45.0% (avg $40.72), London 43.4% ($40.45), New York 52.6% ($49.74), rollover/late-NY 31.0% ($20.67) — shares overlap by construction. Daily range: $29.43 average over 3,178 daily bars since Jan 2015 vs **$90.28 over the last 250 sessions (≈ 3.07×)**. — [GoldHunts](https://goldhunts.com/gold-research/xauusd-volatility-study)
- FXNX "12 consecutive months of raw tick data aggregated into hourly candles" (undated), reporting *median* hourly ATR with IQR because "the mean is distorted by news spikes" (mean overstates baseline by 18–28%): 00:00–07:00 UTC $3.00–$5.50 (FAQ: median ≈ $2.90–$3.50 for 01:00–05:00 and 21:00–23:00); London open $7–$11; **12:30–16:00 UTC $14–$25+** (FAQ: $18.50–$22+); 17:00–23:00 UTC $4–$6. Point estimates: 04:00 UTC $3.10, 05:00 UTC $2.90, 13:30 UTC $21.80, 20:00 UTC median $8.20 / mean $14.90 (mean 81.7% above median because of FOMC days). Median ATR varies "by more than 400%" between Asian consolidation and NY overlap. By weekday at 13:30 UTC: Mon $15.20, Tue $17.80, Wed $19.90, Thu $24.40, Fri $26.80 (Thu/Fri NY hours 35–45% higher than Monday; Fri vs Mon +76%). Spread/ATR: ~10.7% of median hourly ATR at 05:00 UTC vs ~1.4% at 13:30 UTC (assumed raw spread $0.31). A fixed $3 stop ≈ 97–100% of an Asian hourly range but ≈ 13.7% of the NY-overlap hourly range. The article's own numbers are internally inconsistent in places (Asia $3–5.5 vs $2.9–3.5). — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/xauusd-hourly-atr-table-12-months-tick-data)
- Pro-Scalper (educational, no sample stated): typical ranges Asia 10–30 pips, London 30–80 pips, overlap 40–100+ pips; names 08:00–10:00 UTC as the most volatile two-hour window — this contradicts both datasets above, which put the peak at 12:00–15:00 UTC. Pip convention for gold is inconsistent across vendors (FXNX treats $0.10 = 1 pip in one post and $0.01 in another). — [Pro-Scalper](https://www.pro-scalper.com/xauusd-trading-sessions)
- Sobti (2021, *Int. Rev. Fin. Analysis*), 1-minute data 2013–2018: intraday gold price jumps occur in about 0.43% of intervals; US macro news explains about 34% of intraday jumps; FOMC rate decisions are the dominant surprise; gold futures show more intraday jumps than gold ETFs (figures from search snippet; ScienceDirect returned 403). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521921002209)
- Oxford thesis on intraday gold volatility forecasting (period not extracted): highest returns and largest volatility near 12:30 GMT. — [ORA Oxford](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:b52bf91a-06cc-4a90-8063-bf66d365dbf4/files/m953d8281b63afe848d5f634fe367932b)

### Inferences
- Session ratios computed from the GoldHunts table (mean range, 2025–26 regime): Asia 00–07 avg ≈ $14.5/h; London 07–12 ≈ $12.7/h; overlap 12–16 ≈ $21.2/h; NY afternoon 16–21 ≈ $12.1/h. Overlap/Asia ≈ **1.46×**, overlap/London ≈ 1.67×, overlap/NY-afternoon ≈ 1.75×, 13:00 vs 04:00 ≈ 2.3×, 13:00 vs 21:00 ≈ 8.6×. Note that in this sample "Asia" is inflated by 00:00–02:00 UTC (Shanghai/Tokyo open, $17–21/h); the quiet core is 03:00–04:00 UTC ($10–11/h) and the late-London lull 09:00–10:00 UTC ($11–12/h).
- FXNX's median-based ratios (4–5×) and GoldHunts' mean-based ratios (1.5–2.3×) bracket the truth; medians suppress news hours in the overlap, and means in 2025–26 are inflated by a very volatile regime. For stop/target sizing use per-hour *medians* from the bot's own recent data.
- Approximate 1-minute range (needed for an M1 ATR): for a diffusion the expected range scales with √time, so 1-min range ≈ hourly range / √60 ≈ hourly / 7.7. GoldHunts 2025–26: 13:00 UTC $24 → ≈ $3.1 per M1 bar; 04:00 UTC $10.3 → ≈ $1.3; 21:00 UTC $2.8 → ≈ $0.36. FXNX medians: 13:30 UTC $21.8 → ≈ $2.8; 04:00 UTC $3.1 → ≈ $0.40. Real M1 ATR will be somewhat lower than range/√60 because of microstructure (bid-ask bounce raises it; intrabar autocorrelation lowers it); treat ±30%. **These are derived numbers, not measured ones.**
- A "2.5–3 ATR counter-move" therefore means ≈ $8–9 in one to three minutes during the overlap but only ≈ $1–1.2 in mid-Asia; at 0.02 lots (2 oz) that is $16–18 vs $2–2.4 of P&L. The absolute dollar move that would stop out an overlap trade is an ordinary 5-minute swing, not a shock.
- Weekday tilt (FXNX): Thursday/Friday NY hours run 35–76% hotter than Monday at the same hour. If the bot sizes by a single ATR it will be under-sized Monday and over-sized Friday.

### Gaps
- No source publishes a measured 1-minute ATR by UTC hour for XAUUSD or GC; both vendor tables are hourly. The FXNX "24-hour ATR reference table" heading exists but its table body was not present in the fetched text.
- Neither vendor states exact date ranges; the GoldHunts hourly sample is "a few months", so it reflects the 2025–26 regime only.
- No CME Group, LBMA or World Gold Council publication with an hourly volatility/volume profile was found in this pass (CME has hourly volume dashboards but no retrievable study surfaced).

## Key question 3: After an impulse bar ≥2× ATR on M1, probability of continuation vs reversal over the next 5–15 minutes

### Takeaway
No published measurement exists for gold at M1 (or at any intraday horizon with ATR-multiple conditioning). The adjacent evidence points in opposite directions depending on *what caused* the bar: news-driven jumps continue adjusting for more than 5 minutes (FOMC, 5-min data), while fixing-window spikes show no post-event drift and a large majority of M1 breakouts whipsaw (Sönnert's 78% both-sides days). This must be measured locally.

### Cited Findings
- Awartani, Hussain & Virk (2024, *Int. Rev. Fin. Analysis* 95), high-frequency NYMEX/COMEX gold futures around FOMC (sample spans the financial crisis and COVID per keywords): returns and volatility measured 5 minutes after the announcement respond more strongly to looser-than-expected decisions than to tighter; "price and volatility adjustments continue for more than five minutes after the announcement", which the authors read as short-term inefficiency. I.e. *news-driven* impulse bars show continuation beyond 5 minutes. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v95y2024ipbs1057521924004186.html)
- Sobti (2021), 1-min 2013–2018: jumps ≈ 0.43% of minutes; ≈ 34% attributable to US news; the rest unexplained (see Q2 for link). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521921002209)
- Caminschi & Heaney (2014): elevated volume/volatility at the PM-fix start, "no significant impacts or returns following the publication of the fixing results" — a non-news spike with no drift afterwards (via review; not opened). — [BearWorks review](https://bearworks.missouristate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1565&context=articles-cob)
- Sönnert (2015): with ±10 bp from the open, 1,083 of 1,384 days (78.25%) broke both thresholds, i.e. the first breakout reversed enough to hit the opposite side the same day; on the 21.75% one-sided days the move continued to average +0.86%/+0.96%. — [DiVA PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:845497/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- Daily-bar analogue (Nasdaq-100 stocks, 25 years, Wealth-Lab): closes extended by multiple ATRs above a moving average tended to *continue*, not reverse; best results at 7× ATR with ~120-day holds. Not gold, not intraday. — [Backtest Substack](https://backtest.substack.com/p/does-atr-extension-predict-a-reversal)
- Practitioner folklore (Edgeful): once price has used ~80%+ of the daily ATR the day's extremes are "likely set"; a ~2× daily-ATR move "may signal exhaustion". No statistics. — [Edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/average-true-range-trading-strategy)
- Implementation trap noted in a QuantConnect thread: an ATR indicator that updates only once per day goes stale at minute resolution; verify the bot's ATR actually updates every bar. — [QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/19075/mind-the-gap-an-intraday-reversal-strategy-using-gap-downs-and-atr/)

### Inferences
- The evidence splits impulse bars into two populations: (1) scheduled-news bars (FOMC/NFP/CPI, ~⅓ of jumps per Sobti) — measured continuation for >5 min, with direction tied to the surprise sign; (2) unexplained/liquidity bars (~⅔ of jumps) — the fixing-window and ORB evidence suggests these mostly do not drift further and often retrace. A bot that treats all 2× ATR bars identically will mix a continuation population with a reversal population; conditioning on "was there a scheduled release in the last 15 minutes" is the first split to test.
- Because 1-min jumps are rare (0.43% ≈ 6 per 1,440-minute day), a ≥2× M1 ATR bar is not a jump in the statistical sense most of the time; it is the upper tail of ordinary volatility, and under near-random-walk behaviour the expected continuation over the next 5–15 min is close to zero in return terms, while the realised range around the entry stays elevated (volatility clustering). That is the mechanism by which a 2.5–3 ATR counter-move becomes likely right after an impulse: ATR itself is lagging, so the "3 ATR" stop is small relative to just-realised volatility.

### Gaps
- No published probability of continuation vs reversal after ≥2× ATR M1 bars in gold. Recommend measuring on the bot's own M1 database: condition on bar range / ATR(14 or 20) ≥ 2, 2.5, 3; split by UTC hour bucket and by proximity to a scheduled release; measure sign-agreement and MFE/MAE at +5, +10, +15 min, and the share of cases where a counter-move ≥ 2.5 ATR occurs within 15 min.
- Awartani et al. full text (exact sample, magnitudes in USD) not accessible.

## Key question 4: Gold in the hours after NFP, and on Friday afternoons

### Takeaway
NFP: measured evidence shows an inverse, surprise-sign-dependent reaction concentrated in the first 15–60 minutes (≈ $6.5–7.2 average at 15 min in 2023–25; median release-hour range ≈ $41 and first-15-min range ≈ $61 in 2025–26, i.e. 2.3× and 5.9× normal), with the correlation fading by hour 4; the extra volatility is mostly confined to the release hour (release *day* only ~1.1× wider). Friday afternoon "fade/reversal" is pure trader folklore — no backtest found.

### Cited Findings
- FXStreet (July 2025 edition), previous 35 NFP releases (≈ mid-2022 to mid-2025; March-2023 release excluded), XAU/USD measured 15 min, 1 h, 4 h after release vs FXStreet deviation score: 9 negative and 26 positive surprises; average 15-min move +$7.2 after a miss, −$6.5 after a beat; correlation r ≈ −0.54 at 15 min, ≈ −0.54 at 1 h, ≈ −0.42 at 4 h (author: "none close enough to −1 to be considered significant"); author speculates profit-taking into the London PM fix can reverse the initial move a few hours later — stated as a possibility, not measured. — [FXStreet Jul 2025](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-july-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202507311000)
- Same FXStreet series, earlier editions: the inverse relationship weakened to almost nothing by 4 hours (Dec 2021 edition); the 2023 edition reported average rise after misses and fall after beats of similar magnitude (search summary; exact USD not extracted). — [FXStreet Dec 2021](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-december-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-golds-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202201051243); [FXStreet Jan 2023](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-december-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-prices-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202301050939)
- ampeld "Gold on NFP day", COMEX front-month, 20 releases 10 Jan 2025 – 6 Nov 2026: release-hour median range **$41 = 2.3× an ordinary hour**, wider than usual on 17/20 releases; for the 5 releases with 5-min data (Jul–Nov 2026): first-15-min median range **$61 = 5.9×** the same 15 min on other days; first-hour median $62 = 2.9× normal; widest single 5-min candle $105; whole release day only ≈ 1.1× (≈ 15% wider) than preceding ordinary days, i.e. the excess volatility is concentrated at the release. No reversal frequency given ("a wide release hour can end where it began"). Page has internal inconsistencies in its table. — [ampeld](https://ampeld.com/gold-nfp-day)
- A 2026 trading guide reports a median gold range of ≈ $79 in the hour around NFP with the release minute alone moving $12–$81, and that spreads widen then normalise within minutes (vendor, method not stated). — [tradeos](https://www.tradeos.xyz/gold-xauusd-news-trading-strategy-cpi-nfp-2026)
- Folklore on NFP reversal: guides say gold "may initially fall on a strong headline, then reverse higher as traders digest the full report" and recommend waiting 15–30 minutes before acting on the first candle. No statistics. — [Dominion Markets](https://www.dominionmarkets.com/nfp-impact-on-gold/)
- FOMC hour volatility (FXNX): 20:00 UTC (FOMC 19:00 UTC statement hour in US winter / 18:00 summer; FXNX label) median hourly ATR $8.20 vs mean $14.90 — FOMC days roughly double the hour's typical range. — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/xauusd-hourly-atr-table-12-months-tick-data)
- FOMC academic: Awartani et al. 2024 (asymmetric, dovish surprises move gold more; adjustment > 5 min) — see Q3. A Yale 2023 senior essay (1993–2023, daily) finds only marginal significance (p = 0.083) of rate surprises for 2-day gold moves. — [Yale CSEC](https://csec.yale.edu/senior-essays/fall-2023/gold-futures-flux-assessing-impact-unexpected-fomc-decisions)
- Friday: FXNX's weekday table has Friday 13:30 UTC median ATR $26.80, the highest weekday (+76% vs Monday) — but this partly reflects NFP Fridays. — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/xauusd-hourly-atr-table-12-months-tick-data)
- Friday-afternoon folklore (no data in any source): "Friday Fade" (open higher, fade into close), "Afternoon Pullback" (rally in the morning, reverse by 14:00 NY = 19:00 UTC), "sharp intraday reversals, especially after a strong trend throughout the week", institutional desks "closing, not opening positions", position squaring largely done after 10:00 ET. — [Edge-Forex](https://edge-forex.com/trading-gold-on-fridays-why-its-different/); [Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/blog/why-friday-afternoon-matters-more-than-you-think-in-trading); [Databird Journal](https://www.databirdjournal.com/posts/why-gold-trades-differently-than-forex-volatility-patterns-session-timing-and-key-price-levels)

### Inferences
- For a 1-minute scalper the NFP picture is: first 15 minutes have ~6× normal range with directional information (r ≈ −0.5 with surprise), the next 45 minutes still ~3× range with the same sign, and after that the move is statistically indistinguishable from noise. Any "pullback to EMA" trade opened in minutes 1–15 after NFP faces a bar range roughly six times what its ATR (computed from pre-release bars) says; a 3 ATR stop is sub-one-bar.
- NFP-day excess volatility is almost entirely in the release hour (day = 1.1× vs hour = 2.3×), so a blanket "no trading on NFP Friday" rule forfeits more than it saves; a 12:00–14:30 UTC blackout captures the measured excess.
- The Friday-afternoon reversal claim is unmeasured; the one hard Friday number (higher 13:30 UTC ATR) says Friday *US morning* is hotter, not that the afternoon reverses. Treat as untested until checked on local data (compare 16:00–21:00 UTC Friday return autocorrelation and reversal frequency with other weekdays, excluding NFP Fridays).

### Gaps
- No study quantifies how often the initial NFP move in gold fully reverses within the day (ampeld explicitly gives no number).
- No backtest or paper on Friday-afternoon reversal frequency in gold; all sources are narrative.
- No CPI-specific gold intraday study was found in this pass (only NFP and FOMC).

## Key question 5: Failure rate of pullback-to-EMA entries in strong intraday trends vs ranges; what distinguishes a pullback that holds from one that breaks

### Takeaway
No gold-specific or M1-specific measurement exists. Published pullback backtests on other instruments report win rates anywhere from ~36% (short-MA pullbacks with fixed stops/targets on futures) to 82% (daily S&P with 200-day filter); all sources agree failures cluster in chop and late-stage/weak trends, and that a long-period trend filter is what separates a usable from an unusable pullback rule. The 2.5–3 ATR counter-move failure mode specifically is unmeasured anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Quantified Strategies, daily S&P 500: 200-day trend filter + 20-day pullback trigger → 82% win ratio, CAGR 8.3%, max drawdown 30% (COVID). Daily, equities, not gold. — [Quantified Strategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/pullback-trading-strategy/)
- Fractiz "pullback reversion" backtest (futures, short moving average, fixed stop/target): win rates 36.5%, 44.0%, 37.0% across settings, result depends on the stop/target sizes. — [Fractiz](https://www.fractiz.com/strategies/pullback-reversion/)
- Vendor claim (TakeProfit): 55–65% win rate "achievable with trend filtering", dropping to 40–50% without a filter at the same R/R. Not a backtest. — [TakeProfit](https://takeprofitapp.com/en/learn/pullback-trading-strategy)
- Strike.money: pullback entries improve risk-reward but "don't necessarily improve win rates"; failures concentrate "in choppy markets, when price oscillates above and below the slow moving average"; "weak trends, low liquidity, and late-stage moves increase failure risk"; with 2+ warning signs "pullback failure increasingly likely". Qualitative. — [Strike.money](https://www.strike.money/technical-analysis/pullback-trading)
- Backtest hygiene (Backtestify): trend systems typically win under half their trades; the two commonest inflators of pullback backtests are entering on the signal bar (look-ahead) and ignoring fees/slippage. — [Backtestify](https://backtestify.com/blog/moving-average-crossover-backtest)
- Gold-specific context: Sönnert's 78% both-sides days mean that even a very shallow trend (10 bp from the open) reversed through the open on most days — the base rate of "trend fails to hold" intraday in gold is high. — [DiVA PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:845497/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- Spread drag on shallow pullbacks (FXNX): spread ≈ 10.7% of median hourly ATR at 05:00 UTC vs 1.4% at 13:30 UTC; a $3 stop is a full Asian hourly range but 14% of an overlap hour's range. — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/xauusd-hourly-atr-table-12-months-tick-data)

### Inferences
- The one consistent empirical regularity across all pullback sources is regime-dependence: the same rule swings from ~35–45% to ~80% win rate depending on whether a long-horizon trend filter is active. On M1 gold that filter should be at least session-scale (e.g. H1 slope or distance from the daily open), because the hourly data show volatility — and by the news evidence, directional information — switches regime by hour.
- What the evidence says distinguishes holding pullbacks from breaking ones: (1) the trend is young and information-driven (post-news first hour; session open), not late-stage; (2) the move is happening in a high-information window (12:30–16:00 UTC) rather than Asia/post-17:00 "slow chop"; (3) the distance to the MA is small relative to *current* (not lagging) realised range. Nothing in the literature supports a specific EMA length.
- The 2.5–3 ATR counter-move failure is structurally more likely immediately after an impulse because ATR lags: the bar that created the trend inflates realised volatility for the next 5–15 minutes before ATR(14) catches up, so a stop sized at 3 × ATR(14) is ~1–1.5× the range of the bars that just printed. Measuring the ratio (range of last 3 bars) / ATR(14) at entry, and rejecting entries where it exceeds ~2, is the natural filter to test.

### Gaps
- No published failure rate of pullback-to-EMA entries on XAUUSD/GC at any intraday timeframe, and no study of the specific "sudden 2.5–3 ATR counter-move" failure mode. Must be measured on the bot's own trade log and M1 history (classify each entry by hour bucket, H1 trend state, bars-since-impulse; record MAE in ATR units within 15 minutes).
- No evidence on which EMA period (9/20/50) performs differently on gold intraday.

## Cross-cutting measured facts worth carrying into the report (short list, with regime dates)

- Gold's daily range tripled: $29.43/day (2015–2026 average, 3,178 bars) vs $90.28/day (last 250 sessions as of 2025–26) — [GoldHunts](https://goldhunts.com/gold-research/xauusd-volatility-study). Every USD-denominated stop, target or ATR threshold older than ~12 months is stale.
- Hourly range peak 12:00–15:00 UTC ($21–24/h), trough 21:00 UTC ($2.80/h), mid-Asia 03:00–04:00 UTC ($10–11/h), London 07:00–12:00 UTC ($11–14/h) — same source, 2025–26 regime.
- Median hourly ATR Asia ≈ $3 vs overlap ≈ $18–22 (earlier 12-month tick sample, >4× ratio) — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/xauusd-hourly-atr-table-12-months-tick-data).
- 1-min jumps ≈ 0.43% of minutes, ~34% explained by US news, FOMC dominant (1-min data 2013–2018) — [Sobti 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521921002209).
- FOMC: gold keeps adjusting > 5 min after the statement, more on dovish surprises (5-min data) — [Awartani et al. 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v95y2024ipbs1057521924004186.html).
- NFP: ±$6.5–7.2 average at 15 min with r ≈ −0.54 vs surprise (35 releases to mid-2025); first-15-min range 5.9× normal, release hour 2.3×, day 1.1× (2025–26) — [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/us-july-nonfarm-payrolls-preview-analyzing-gold-price-reaction-to-nfp-surprises-202507311000); [ampeld](https://ampeld.com/gold-nfp-day).
- Opening-range momentum in COMEX gold 2009–2014: 78% of days whipsaw through a ±10 bp range, 22% trend ≈ +0.9%; ORB Sharpe 1.28 vs 0.34 buy-and-hold — [Sönnert 2015](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:845497/FULLTEXT01.pdf).
- NY futures lead price discovery on average over 17 years of intraday data despite < 10% of London's volume — [Hauptfleisch, Putniņš & Lucey 2016](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jfutmk:v:36:y:2016:i:6:p:564-586).
