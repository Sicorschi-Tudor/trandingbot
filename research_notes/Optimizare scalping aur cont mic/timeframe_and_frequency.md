# Timeframe and trade frequency for a ~100 USD gold account: M1 scalping vs M5/M15 session setups

Scope note: searches covered MQL5 blogs/forum/Market, TradingView, PLoS ONE / academic theses, Unger Academy (CME gold futures), Substack quant blogs, GitHub and broker education pages. Almost every number below comes from a party with a commercial interest (EA vendors, brokers, education firms) or from a single-author backtest; I found **no peer-reviewed or fully independent M1-vs-M5-vs-M15 comparison on gold**. Treat every win rate / PF figure as "reported", not verified. Times are given as the source gives them (UTC/GMT vs broker server time is flagged each time; most MT5 brokers run server time = UTC+2/+3, so "08:00 server" ≈ 05:00–06:00 UTC, i.e. the London pre-open).

## Key question 1: What spread-to-target ratios make M1 gold scalping viable, and what do backtests show for M1 vs M5 vs M15 versions of the same strategy?

### Takeaway
No source ran the same pullback system on M1, M5 and M15 with published numbers; the only evidence is cost arithmetic plus vendor backtests, and all of it points the same way: at a 2 USD target the 0.2–0.3 USD gold spread is already 10–15 % of the target before slippage, which forces a break-even win rate above ~56 %, whereas 5–15 USD targets drop cost drag to 2–5 %. Vendor M1 scalpers that report 80 %+ win rates do so on fixed-spread tick models that an MQL5 author explicitly says "can flatter thin-take-profit scalpers".

### Cited Findings
- Spread share of target (35-cent spread example from a gold-EA vendor blog): 1.00 USD target → 35 % of the edge; 3.00 USD target → ~12 %; 10 USD+ target → ~3 %. Per-trade cost at 0.10 lot with 35-cent spread ≈ 3.50 USD; an EA doing 4 trades/day at 0.10 lot pays ≈ 70 USD/week in spread alone. A backtest run with a fixed 20-cent spread "can show profits that disappear on a live account with a 45-cent spread". — [dev.to / xauusdrobot, "The XAUUSD spread: the hidden cost"](https://dev.to/xauusdrobot/the-xauusd-spread-the-hidden-cost-that-quietly-decides-your-trading-results-574d)
- Typical gold spreads by account type: raw/ECN 10–35 cents + commission; standard accounts 30–50 cents; rollover (~21:00–23:00 GMT) spreads jump 3–10×; Asian session "persistently wider"; London–NY overlap tightest; NFP/CPI/FOMC "several dollars for minutes". — [dev.to / xauusdrobot](https://dev.to/xauusdrobot/the-xauusd-spread-the-hidden-cost-that-quietly-decides-your-trading-results-574d)
- Spread cost at 0.01 lot on a 100 USD account: typical retail markup 2.0–3.0 "pips" = 0.20–0.30 USD per trade; a raw account example = 0.15 USD all-in (0.07 commission + 0.08 markup). Cost drag at 0.02 lot with a wide-spread broker ≈ 6–12 % of starting capital per month "before trade outcomes". — [FXNX, "What $100 can trade in gold"](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/what-100-trade-gold-margin-lot-size-honest-ceiling)
- Live-vs-backtest spread reality on XAUUSD M15 session breakout: backtest used a fixed 18-point spread; live, the 13:30 UTC news window showed 60/90/140-point spreads for 3–5 minutes, a "calm Tuesday 14:52 UTC" showed a 110-point spread, March-2022 NFP went 22→108 points in under a minute; entry slippage 5–8 points from a 30–40 ms routing gap; account was −11 % after three weeks live. — [MQL5 blog, Gold Algo Lab, London/NY overlap post](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770488)
- Vendor M1 scalper results (self-reported, MT5 tester): one EA reports 87.11 % win rate over 13,491 trades, PF 4.72, tested at a 100-point spread while its own live spread filter defaults to 30 points; another reports 71.91 % win, 3,229 trades, PF 1.27 (IC Markets, 25-point max spread filter); a third shows 15 wins / 5 losses only. — [MQL5 Market 174156](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/174156); [MQL5 Market 180216](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/180216); [MQL5 Market 175093](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/175093)
- A TradingView 1-minute gold strategy author states that "with realistic costs applied, expect win rate ≈ 55–70 %, profit factor > 1.4–1.8 in favorable periods" (no methodology shown). — [TradingView 1-minute scripts listing](https://in.tradingview.com/scripts/1minute)
- Independent-style survey of 43 paid gold EAs (Jan 2015–Jul 2026, MetaQuotes data, M15 chart, 1-minute OHLC price model, default inputs, 10,000 USD): 40 % had PF > 2 and equity DD < 15 %; 26 % profitable with DD < 40 %; 19 % of measurable EAs hit equity DD of 60–102 %. The author notes a 1-minute OHLC model "can flatter thin-take-profit scalpers" and that balance drawdown can understate equity drawdown by up to 4× on grid systems; the most expensive EA (1,999 USD) had PF 0.23 and 99 % DD. Author sells EAs. — [MQL5 blog 773167, "We backtested 43 popular gold EAs"](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/773167)
- Forum rule of thumb for required history: "some months for M1 scalping strategies, about 1 year for M5 scalping strategies, 1–2 years at least for M15–M30"; an MT5 thread documents a stop-out that appeared on M15 but not on M1 because ticks are generated from M1 data — same EA, different results by timeframe. — [MQL5 forum 324468](https://mql5.com/en/forum/324468/13597256); [MQL5 forum 44495](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/44495)
- Vendor (H4 gold EA seller) argument that "lower timeframes are dominated by spread, slippage, and broker execution quality" and that "adapting a M1 scalper to H4 doesn't work, and vice versa" — i.e. a rule set rescaled to another timeframe is a different system. — [MQL5 blog 770260](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770260)
- MQL5 forum thread "Is this backtest worth pursuing" (XAUUSD, M10 chart with M5/M1 confirmation, real ticks 99 %): original 4.5-month run on 100 USD at 1:1000 leverage showed PF 1.52, win 68.31 %, 284 trades, equity DD 46.16 %; the 29-month fixed-lot retest (Jan 2024–May 2026, 1,631 trades) fell to PF 1.34, balance DD 32 %, 22 of 29 months profitable, max 14 consecutive losses. Responders: test fixed lots in points without MM, test choppy/downtrend regimes, 5 months is too short, a 46 % equity DD is a margin-call risk. — [MQL5 forum 509738](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/509738)
- Academic microstructure (5-minute gold data, May 2000–Apr 2015, 1.08 M observations): relative bid-ask spread fell from 0.00185 (2000–05) to 0.00060 (2010–15); spread is roughly constant through the day, rising slightly around 22:00 GMT (daily closure 21:00–22:00 GMT); volume is "n-shaped", peaking 11:00–17:00 GMT (Europe/US overlap); volatility flat until ~12:00 GMT, rising to ~14:00 GMT, then declining. — [Batten et al., PLoS ONE 2017 (PMC5407636)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5407636/)

### Inferences
- Cost arithmetic for this account (my derivation, spread paid once per round trip, no slippage): spread 0.25 USD vs 2 USD target = 12.5 % of target; vs 5 USD = 5 %; vs 10 USD = 2.5 %; vs 15 USD = 1.7 %. Adding 0.10 USD slippage (the 5–8 points reported live) pushes the 2 USD-target drag to ~17 %.
- Break-even win rate with symmetric target/stop T=S and total cost c, from E = p·T − (1−p)·S − c = 0 → p* = (S + c)/(T + S): at T=S=2, c=0.25 → 56.3 %; c=0.35 → 58.8 %. At T=S=10, c=0.25 → 51.3 %. So an M1 scalper needs ~5–8 percentage points more win rate than a 10 USD-target system just to stand still, before any API/"per-call" cost.
- Daily cost drag at 0.01 lot: 10 trades/day × 0.25 USD = 2.5 USD/day ≈ 2.5 % of a 100 USD account per day, ≈ 50 USD (50 %) per 20-day month; 2 trades/day = 0.5 USD/day ≈ 10 USD (10 %)/month. This matches the FXNX "6–12 %/month before outcomes" figure at 0.02 lot and low frequency.
- Fixed per-trade/per-call costs (e.g. an LLM API call per decision, or commission) behave exactly like extra spread: they are independent of target size, so they fall as a share of expectancy only when target per trade rises or calls per trade fall. If a decision costs x USD and the trade's net edge is E, the system is viable only if E > x; a 2 USD-target system with 0.3 USD net edge per trade cannot carry even 0.10 USD per decision at a 3:1 call-to-trade ratio.
- Trade frequency vs variance: with per-trade expectancy e and st.dev. s, daily mean = n·e and daily st.dev. = s·√n, so more trades improve the daily Sharpe by √n **only if e stays positive after costs**; at M1 the cost-adjusted e is the smallest and most fragile, so extra frequency mostly adds variance and spread drag.
- The 43-EA survey and the forum thread both show the same pattern: short windows (5 months, Jan–Apr 2025) give PF 1.5–3+, extending to 2+ years pulls PF down to 1.3–1.4 and reveals 30–46 % drawdowns. Expect the same shrinkage from any M1 result.

### Gaps
- No published backtest runs the same EMA-pullback (or any) gold strategy on M1, M5 and M15 side by side with costs; the question "what do backtests show for M1 vs M5 vs M15 versions of the same pullback strategy" has no documented answer. The only data points are indirect (forum anecdotes, vendor opinion).
- No source gives measured average XAUUSD spread per UTC hour for 2024–2026; figures are broker marketing ranges.
- Win rate was not reported for most session EAs; PF and DD are the only consistently reported stats.

## Key question 2: Which session-based gold strategies have published backtest statistics, and on which hours do they work?

### Takeaway
The best-documented session strategy is the Asian-range → London-open breakout: a 3.7-year real-tick MT5 test (Jan 2023–Sep 2026) gives PF 1.37–1.38, ~49 % win rate, 17–19 % max DD at 1 % risk, 715 trades, with PF 1.61 on 157 out-of-sample 2026 trades; a second M15 session-breakout EA (03:00–08:00 server range) reports PF 1.61 / DD 29 % (conservative) over Jan 2025–Jan 2026. London–NY-overlap breakout (12:00–17:00 UTC) and NY-afternoon mean reversion have rules published but no PF/DD statistics; CME-futures mean-reversion at the previous day's high/low (Unger Academy) reports ~100–190 USD/trade average but withholds trade counts, DD and PF.

### Cited Findings
**Asian range → London open breakout (XAUUSD)**
- Rules: Asian range = high/low of 03:00–08:00 broker server time; at 08:00 place Buy Stop above / Sell Stop below (OCO, small buffer); SL = opposite side of range, capped at 0.6 % of price; TP = 2.5R, stop to break-even at 1.5R; flat at 21:00 server; one trade/day; skip days where range < 0.30 % or > 1.60 % of price. — [MQL5 blog 777540, "London Breakout on Gold: 3.7 years of backtests"](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- Results (MT5 real ticks, 5,000 USD start): Broker A 1 % risk: +6,860 USD, PF 1.38, max DD 17.3 %, 715 trades, ~49 % win; Broker B: +6,619 USD, PF 1.37, DD 18.8 %, 716 trades; 0.5 % risk: PF 1.32, DD 11.2 %; 2026 out-of-sample (settings chosen on 2023–2025 only): PF 1.61 on 157 trades ("a small sample"). Spread/commission not stated. — [MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- What failed in that test: tight stops inside the range ("normal noise hit them too often"); no range filter (more false breakouts); holding past NY close (swap + gap risk, no gain); 5R target (PF fell to 1.30, DD rose). Author calls 15–19 % DD "normal at 1 % risk" and suggests 0.3–0.4 % risk for 10 %-max-loss prop accounts. Forward test on demo "underway". — [MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- Second session-range breakout EA, XAUUSD M15, Jan 2025–Jan 2026, 540 USD start, 2,507 optimisation passes: conservative set (range 03:00–08:00 server, 6.6 % risk/trade, max 2 trades/day, news filter ON, EOD close ON) PF 1.61, max DD 29.44 %, 59 trades; aggressive set (04:00–09:00, 4.7 % risk, up to 8 trades/day, news filter OFF) PF 2.71, DD 56.41 %, 45 trades, +7,013 USD (≈156 USD/trade derived). Live 54 days: 310 USD → 851 USD peak, reported max DD 0.39 % (inconsistent with the 1,000 USD withdrawal claim). Author notes "small shifts in the range start hour noticeably change trade quality" and recommends a raw-spread ECN account. — [MQL5 blog 767519](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/767519)
- TradingView open-source version: Asian range 01:00–08:00 server, buy on London close above Asian high + ATR buffer, sell below low − buffer, one signal per direction per day; no statistics. — [TradingView "GOLD Asian Range Breakout Signals"](https://kr.tradingview.com/script/rCMX1vGE-GOLD-Asian-Range-Breakout-Signals/)
- Unsupported claim repeated in vendor material: the London breakout direction "holds for the day approximately 65–70 % of the time" (no dataset cited). — [MQL5 blog 768254](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/768254)

**London–NY overlap breakout (12:00–17:00 UTC)**
- Rules published (no PF/DD/win rate): M15 close outside the London-session range with wick-to-body ratio < 25 %; daily range must be < 70 % of 14-day ATR; enter on first pullback candle, stop below the range edge; exit before 17:00 UTC unless strong continuation; news pause T−5 to T+15 min around high-impact events; abort if spread above threshold; validate on demo for ≥ 60 sessions tracking slippage separately. Author's own live result: −11 % in three weeks after a 6-month fixed-18-point-spread backtest. — [MQL5 blog 770488](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770488)
- Broker/education consensus: 13:00–17:00 UTC is the most active window and has the tightest spreads (0.2–0.5 "pips" quoted vs 1.5–4 in Asia); Asian-session range often "20–40 pips"; US CPI/NFP/FOMC moves of 50–200 pips within minutes. All qualitative, promotional sources. — [dev.to / xauusdrobot sessions post](https://dev.to/xauusdrobot/when-does-gold-actually-move-xauusd-trading-sessions-explained-i4a); [TMGM gold trading hours](https://www.tmgm.com/es/academy/trading-academy/gold-trading-hours)
- Academic confirmation of the volume/volatility window: gold volume peaks ~11:00–17:00 GMT and volatility peaks ~12:00–14:00 GMT (2000–2015, 5-min data). — [Batten et al., PLoS ONE 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5407636/)

**Mean reversion at previous-day high/low (CME gold futures, Unger Academy)**
- Strategy 1: 15-min bars; long after price breaks below the previous day's low and comes back above it ("fake breakout"); short mirror at the previous day's high; flat by end of day. Reported: ~135 USD average trade (full GC contract, 100 oz), "very good" out-of-sample since 2018, 80 % profitability in 2022; costs "net of fees and slippage" but no amounts; no trade count, DD, PF, win rate, stop or session hours given. Strategy 2 (longs on 5-min, shorts on 15-min bars): ~100 USD average trade, weak first out-of-sample years, "short side suffered much more". — [Unger Academy, "Intraday strategies on gold that trade against the trend"](https://ungeracademy.com/blog/intraday-strategies-on-gold-that-trade-against-the-trend-or-or-rules-performance)
- Other Unger videos cite ~650 trades averaging 144 USD, and > 2,000 trades averaging 192 USD with > 35,000 USD over two years net of commissions/slippage (longs on break of previous day's low, shorts on break of previous day's high); the firm claims gold "developed mean-reverting characteristics since 2017–2018" and concedes one short-side version "failed to excel out-of-sample" during gold rallies. — [Unger Academy, "+$35,000 profit in 2 years (mean reverting + bias)"](https://ungeracademy.com/?p=6851); [Unger Academy, "Intraday strategies on gold"](https://ungeracademy.com/?p=6696)
- Unger trend strategy (60-min 110-period MA on a 5-min build, SL 1,200 USD, TP 650 USD per GC contract, flat by EOD) is the "low capitalisation" example only because Micro Gold (MGC, 1/10 of GC) exists; no stats. — [Unger Academy, "Strategies on gold that are working (also for low capitalization)"](https://ungeracademy.com/blog/strategies-on-gold-that-are-working-also-for-low-capitalization-structure-and-real-performance)

**Intraday seasonality / "first hour" and NY afternoon**
- A CBS master's thesis reports a "hat-shaped" intraday seasonality: gold appreciates robustly during eastern (Asian) trading hours and depreciates for the rest of the day, linked to eastern imports and the London fix (the PDF text was not extractable; hours and magnitudes could not be verified). — [CBS, Donati & Jung, "Gold price dynamics around the clock"](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/gold-price-dynamics-around-the-clock/)
- A Zurich thesis finds gold mean returns around the London AM and PM fix "show the highest negative values of the whole trading day". — [UZH publication 11299](https://www.merlin.uzh.ch/publication/show/11299)
- A vendor "bias" pattern quoted by Benzinga: gold "often weakens overnight and rebounds in the afternoon"; a 60-min illustration uses long entries 10:00 am and short entries 2:00 am exchange time. Not a tested rule set. — [Benzinga, "Uncovering hidden biases in gold futures"](https://benzinga.com/z/30206562)
- Hourly gold-futures breakout (Unger, CME GC): strong 2010–2014, "gradually decreased" since — illustrates regime decay of breakout systems on gold. — [Unger Academy breakout on hourly bars](https://ungeracademy.com/?p=6615)
- Generic ORB evidence: Quantified Strategies' own backtests conclude ORB "has become almost completely obsolete" (index-focused); a methodology-sound ORB test with 200-day MA filter exists only for micro-Nasdaq futures. — [Quantified Strategies, ORB](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-trading-strategies); [Backtests Not Signals, ORB](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge)

### Inferences
- Converting server time: a 03:00–08:00 server range on a UTC+2/+3 broker is ~00:00/01:00–05:00/06:00 UTC, i.e. the breakout fires in the London pre-open (06:00–07:00 UTC), before the 07:00–08:00 UTC London cash open. The volume/volatility academic data (peak 11:00–17:00 GMT) explain why the same authors close everything by NY close and why 12:00–17:00 UTC is the alternative breakout window.
- The two session-breakout tests converge on PF ≈ 1.3–1.6 and ~49 % win rate with 2.5R targets over multi-year samples; the PF 2.7 variant only appears with 4.7–6.6 % risk per trade, ≤ 1-year data and 56 % DD. A realistic planning figure for a well-filtered session breakout on gold is PF 1.3–1.4, win ≈ 45–50 %, R ≈ 2–2.5, ≈ 1 trade/day.
- Translating the 0.6 % stop cap to USD at ~4,000 USD/oz gold = ~24 USD stop at 0.01 lot — i.e. a session breakout on a 100 USD account risks ~24 % per trade at the minimum lot, unless the stop is tightened (which the author found fails) or gold is cheaper. This is the central constraint for a 100 USD account (see Q4).
- Previous-day-high/low mean reversion has the most consistent anecdotal support (Unger: positive average trades net of costs, out-of-sample since 2018), but every statistic comes from a course vendor and trade counts/DD are missing; the M5/M15 execution timeframe used there matches the user's "fewer, higher-quality setups" camp rather than M1.

### Gaps
- No win-rate/PF/DD statistics anywhere for a London–NY overlap breakout or a NY-afternoon mean-reversion rule on gold; only rules and qualitative claims.
- Session hours in MQL5 posts are broker server time with the UTC offset unstated; exact UTC windows cannot be pinned down beyond ±1–2 h.
- The CBS "hat-shaped" seasonality and the Zurich fix-time finding could not be read in detail (PDF unreadable, abstract-only).

## Key question 3: Does reducing trade frequency to the 2–4 best setups per day improve expectancy per trade enough to offset fewer trades, in documented tests?

### Takeaway
No controlled test on gold documents "same strategy, filtered to the N best setups". The closest evidence is indirect: range filters and max-trades-per-day caps were part of every profitable multi-year session system found (1 trade/day in the 3.7-year test; 2/day in the PF 1.61 conservative set vs 8/day in the 56 %-DD aggressive set), and a stochastic-extremes gold system found a liquidity-hours filter removed 10–25 % of trades "without changing results much", i.e. filtering raised per-trade quality roughly proportionally.

### Cited Findings
- 3.7-year London breakout: the final rule set limits to one trade/day and skips ~range < 0.30 % or > 1.60 % days; removing the range filter produced "more false breakouts and Asian-session moves" (worse results, numbers not given). — [MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- M15 session EA: max 2 trades/day + news filter → PF 1.61, DD 29 %; max 8 trades/day, no news filter → PF 2.71 but DD 56 %, on 45 vs 59 trades in one year; the news filter "avoided several poor candles on NFP and CPI days". — [MQL5 blog 767519](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/767519)
- Stochastic-extremes gold mean-reversion (2H bars, CFD, Apr 2006–Aug 2025, OOS 2021→; 0.5-point spread, 20,000 USD): CAGR 4.98 %, max DD 8.8 %, win 68.95 %, PF 1.65, avg gain 64 EUR/trade; a session filter avoiding the lowest-liquidity hours "removed 10–25 % of trades without changing results much". — [Algomatic Trading, "Strategy 7: mean reversion gold"](https://algomatictrading.substack.com/p/strategy-7-a-mean-reversion-gold)
- Unger mean-reversion variant: 85 trades (~40/year) since Sep 2021 for ≈ 20,000 USD reported profit on GC — very low frequency, high average trade (~235 USD/trade derived), self-reported. — [Unger Academy ?p=6867](https://ungeracademy.com/?p=6867)
- MQL5 forum critique of the 284-trade M10/M5/M1 system: responders asked for a market-structure filter "to stop counter-trend entries rather than relying only on a cooldown after losses", and noted the equity curve went sideways after ~trade 118 (weak for under two-thirds of the period). Extending the sample 5 → 29 months reduced PF 1.52 → 1.34. — [MQL5 forum 509738](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/509738)
- Gold archetype critique: trend/breakout systems on gold suffer "convincing breakouts that reverse within hours"; pullback-buying suffers gaps/spikes that fill "far below the planned level, past the stop"; mean reversion "earns steadily during long ranges, then loses much of its gains" when gold trends for months — any archetype looks good if the window excludes its failure regime. — [MQL5 blog 774959, "Why gold breaks most EA archetypes"](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/774959)
- Prop-firm style guidance: at 0.5 % risk per trade and a 1 % daily cap you get two trades per day, "forcing a third trade … is how funded traders breach the daily limit"; general small-account guides recommend 1–2 trades/day. — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/what-100-trade-gold-margin-lot-size-honest-ceiling); [Traders Union, profitability of gold trading](https://tradersunion.com/interesting-articles/how-to-trade-gold-online/profitability-of-gold-trading)

### Inferences
- Across the documented systems, the ones that survived > 2 years all trade ~0.5–1 time per day with filters; the ones showing PF > 2.5 either trade more per day with 4–7 % risk and short samples, or are M1 scalpers on fixed-spread tick models. This is consistent with (but does not prove) the hypothesis that filtering to few setups preserves expectancy while cutting cost drag and variance.
- The frequency trade-off expressed in numbers for this account: 10 M1 trades/day at 2 USD target and a generous 58 % win rate with 0.25 USD cost gives E ≈ 0.58·2 − 0.42·2 − 0.25 = +0.07 USD/trade → ~0.7 USD/day; 2 session trades/day at 10 USD target, 10 USD stop, 45 % win, 0.25 USD cost gives E = 4.5 − 5.5 − 0.25 = −1.25 (loses) — so a 1:1 R:R session trade needs ≥ 52 % win; at 2.5R (TP 12.5, SL 5), 45 % win: E = 5.6 − 2.75 − 0.25 = +2.6 USD/trade → ~2.6–5 USD/day on 1–2 trades. The low-frequency system wins by ~4–7× per day on these assumptions, and does so with 1/5 the spread outlay. These are my derivations from the cited PF/win-rate ranges, not reported results.
- The news-filter and range-filter effects reported qualitatively map directly onto "fewer, better setups": both remove trades taken in regimes (spike/news, dead range) where the gold archetype critique says the archetype fails.

### Gaps
- No documented test ranks setups by a quality score and compares "top 2–4 per day" vs "all signals" on gold; the effect size of selectivity is undocumented.
- Win rates for the filtered vs unfiltered variants were not reported by either MQL5 author, so expectancy per trade before/after filtering cannot be computed from their data.

## Key question 4: What do reputable sources recommend for accounts under 200 USD on gold: timeframe, trades/day, target/stop in USD?

### Takeaway
Every source that does the arithmetic concludes a 100 USD account is below the structural minimum for gold at 0.01 lot: a typical 15–25 USD structural stop is 15–25 % of the account, the 2026 average daily range at 0.01 lot equals ~120 USD (12 % of a 1,000 USD account), and EA vendors' own minimums are 600–1,000 USD (or a cent account). The consistent recommendations are: 0.01–0.02 lot ceiling, 1–2 trades/day, hourly/structural stops of 15–20 USD, and avoiding stops inside noise; none recommends M1 scalping for small accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Exposure and margin (22 Oct 2026, gold 4,024 USD, IC Markets feed): 0.01 lot = 1 oz = 1 USD P&L per 1 USD move; margin for 0.01 lot = 201 USD at 1:20, 40 USD at 1:100, 8 USD at 1:500 (1:20 is the EU/AU retail CFD limit). Average daily high-to-low range on 0.01 lot vs a 1,000 USD account: 3.30 % (2024) → 12.05 % average / 10.0 % median (2026 to 22 Oct), i.e. "about 3.6 times as much money in an average day". A 25 USD stop on 0.01 lot risks 25 USD regardless of leverage. — [MQL5 blog 779060, "What 0.01 lot of gold means now"](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/779060)
- 100 USD account guidance (gold ~2,600 USD): structural stops on hourly charts "usually sit 15–20 USD from price"; at 0.01 lot a 20 USD stop = 20 % of the account; "your operational ceiling on a 100 USD deposit is strictly 0.02 lots"; if a setup needs a stop wider than 20 USD use 0.01 or stay in cash; daily ATR 25–40 USD; stop-out at 50 % margin level means equity 13 USD; at 1:100 and 0.01 lot a ~87 USD adverse move triggers stop-out; at 0.03 lot a typical 30 USD daily range can cost 90–120 USD; cost drag 6–12 %/month at 0.02 lot with a wide-spread broker; "stops placed inside normal noise get hit repeatedly even when the direction is correct". No trades/day figure (examples use 5 trades/week, 10/month). — [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/what-100-trade-gold-margin-lot-size-honest-ceiling)
- EA-vendor minimums: The Gold Reaper lists 600 USD minimum; a free gold strategy has an average stop ≈ 18 USD at 0.01 lot and a worst historical losing streak ≈ 138 USD (14 % of 1,000 USD) → author suggests ≥ 1,000 USD or a cent account; stress test (8 strategies at 0.01 lot, 1,000 Monte-Carlo runs) 95th-percentile DD ≈ 574 USD = 95.6 % of a 600 USD account, 57.4 % of 1,000, 28.7 % of 2,000; ~3,800 USD needed to keep stress DD under 15 %. — [MQL5 blog 776723, "How much money do you need to trade gold with an EA?"](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776723)
- Risk-per-trade arithmetic at 100 USD: "risking 2 % of 100 USD means you can only afford a 2 USD loss per trade", which is tight for gold's swings; gold spreads "often 20–50 pips (0.20–0.50 USD per micro lot)". — [Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/interesting-articles/how-to-trade-gold-online/profitability-of-gold-trading); [Exness blog via issuu](https://issuu.com/exness_blog/docs/exness.docx/s/98363193)

### Inferences
- At gold ≈ 4,000 USD/oz, 0.01 lot and a 100 USD account, the "2 % risk" rule gives a 2 USD stop — which is 8× the spread and well inside the noise the FXNX and MQL5 authors say gets "hit repeatedly". A structurally placed stop (15–25 USD) is 15–25 % risk per trade. The 100 USD account is therefore forced into one of three choices: (a) accept 15–25 % risk per trade on 1 setup/day (ruin risk high: four consecutive losses ≈ −60 %), (b) trade a cent account / 0.001-lot equivalent, or (c) accept noise-level 2–5 USD stops and the ~56–59 % break-even win rate from Q1.
- The M1 "2 USD target / ~2 USD stop" design is the only one whose stop fits a 2 % rule on 100 USD — which is likely why small accounts gravitate to it — but it is also the design with the worst cost ratio (12–17 %) and the one that sources say gets stopped by noise and spread spikes (110-point spreads on calm days = 1.10 USD ≈ half the stop).
- A compromise consistent with the sources: 1–2 setups/day on M5/M15 at session times with 5–8 USD stops and 10–15 USD targets (≈ 2R), risking 5–8 % per trade — still aggressive, but with cost drag ≈ 2–5 % of target and stops outside M1 noise. No source states this exact recipe; it is my synthesis.

### Gaps
- No reputable source gives a specific "timeframe + trades/day + target/stop" recipe for < 200 USD on gold; the recurring answer is "too small — use a cent account or ≥ 600–1,000 USD".
- No data on how a 2026-level daily range (~120 USD at 0.01 lot) changes the required stop versus 2024; only the 3.6× range increase is documented.

## Key question 5: Evidence on mean reversion at the extremes of the Asian range or the previous day's range for gold intraday?

### Takeaway
The only quantified evidence is vendor-reported (Unger Academy on CME gold futures): fading false breaks of the previous day's high/low on 5–15 min bars with end-of-day exit shows positive average trades of ~100–190 USD per 100-oz contract net of costs, with out-of-sample performance since 2018 and a weaker short side. Asian-range-extreme fades have scripts but no statistics, and academic work shows only that returns are weakest around the London fixes and that the Asian session is the quietest/widest-spread window; an MQL5 author warns gold mean reversion fails "near-certainly over a long window" when gold trends.

### Cited Findings
- Previous-day H/L fade (CME GC, 15-min bars, flat by EOD): ≈ 135 USD average trade, OOS since 2018 "very good", 80 % profitability in 2022; 5-min-long / 15-min-short variant ≈ 100 USD average trade, weak early OOS years, shorts "suffered much more". No trade count, DD, PF, win rate. — [Unger Academy, counter-trend intraday gold](https://ungeracademy.com/blog/intraday-strategies-on-gold-that-trade-against-the-trend-or-or-rules-performance)
- Same family: ~650 trades averaging 144 USD; > 2,000 trades averaging 192 USD and > 35,000 USD in two years "net of commissions and slippage"; claim that gold became mean-reverting "since 2017–2018, perhaps helped by high liquidity"; one short-side version "failed to excel out-of-sample". — [Unger Academy ?p=6851](https://ungeracademy.com/?p=6851); [Unger Academy ?p=6696](https://ungeracademy.com/?p=6696)
- Stochastic-extremes mean reversion on gold (2H, 2006–2025, OOS 2021→): win 68.95 %, PF 1.65, DD 8.8 %, CAGR 4.98 % on 20,000 USD; author says also profitable on 1H and 4H and stable across parameters (unverifiable; code paywalled). — [Algomatic Trading](https://algomatictrading.substack.com/p/strategy-7-a-mean-reversion-gold)
- Asian-range / previous-day-level liquidity-sweep reversal: TradingView "1-Min Gold Taylor Technique" and similar scripts mark Asian range and PDH/PDL and treat sweeps as false-break triggers — visual tools, no performance data. — [TradingView Asian scripts search](https://es.tradingview.com/scripts/asian)
- Bollinger mean reversion on XAUUSD M15: vendor "SAL-V03 Gold Reversion Pro" reports ≈ +5 % backtest and optimised returns and warns of "30 %+ drawdown potential"; an MQL5 forum user reported poor one-year XAUUSD M15 results for a Bollinger EA and others concurred; a vendor advertising an "89–90 % structural win-rate advantage" for mean reversion backtested on EURUSD, not gold. — [cTrader product 3199](https://ctrader.com/en/products/3199); [MQL5 forum 509738](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/509738); [MQL5 Market 176897](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/176897)
- Regime warning: mean reversion on gold "earns steadily while gold ranges", with failure "rare in any given window and near-certain over a long one"; demand tests spanning the post-2011 bear years and choppy ranges, not just 2023-onward. — [MQL5 blog 774959](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/774959)
- Academic: gold mean returns around the London AM/PM fixes show "the highest negative values of the whole trading day" (Zurich thesis); "hat-shaped" intraday seasonality with appreciation during eastern hours and depreciation after (CBS thesis); volume peaks 11:00–17:00 GMT and spread rises around 22:00 GMT (Batten et al.). — [UZH 11299](https://www.merlin.uzh.ch/publication/show/11299); [CBS thesis](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/gold-price-dynamics-around-the-clock/); [PLoS ONE 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5407636/)
- "Reversal day" pattern tested on GLD ETF (not spot/futures): ≈ 1.5 % average gain with 20-day exit, PF > 1.8, small signal count — swing horizon, not intraday. — [Pulse.bot reversal day](https://www.pulse.bot/stock-trading/blogs/reversal-day-strategy-for-the-markets-1fbb2118-59a3-415b-8f16-28b75dbc8c47)

### Inferences
- The previous-day-range fade is the one mean-reversion design with multi-year positive out-of-sample anecdotes on gold; it also executes on M5/M15 with one or two triggers a day, which fits the "few, high-quality setups" camp. Scaled to 0.01 lot (1 oz vs 100 oz), the reported 100–190 USD/contract average becomes ~1–1.9 USD per trade before retail spread (≈ 0.25 USD) — i.e. roughly 0.75–1.65 USD net per trade on a 100 USD account, which is positive but thin, and the Unger numbers include no trade count or DD to judge variance.
- The academic fix-time weakness (negative returns around 10:30 and 15:00 London) and the Asian-hours appreciation are candidate seasonal filters but have not been shown to be tradeable after a 0.2–0.3 USD spread at retail; the thesis-level work tests with and without transaction costs but its results were not readable here.
- Asian-range-extreme fades specifically (sell the Asian high / buy the Asian low during Asia) have zero published statistics; given that the Asian session has the widest spreads and smallest range (20–40 "pips" claimed), the cost-to-target ratio there is the worst of the day.

### Gaps
- No PF, win rate, drawdown or trade count for any previous-day-range or Asian-range fade on spot XAUUSD; the Unger GC numbers are the only quantified ones and are self-reported with no sample size.
- No independent replication of the "gold became mean-reverting after 2017–2018" claim.
- No reproducible GitHub/Python M5/M15 gold mean-reversion backtest with published results was found (soloshun/Quantitative-XAUUSD-Strategy is H1-only; wg722/xauusd-backtest is an engine without results; andywarui/xaubot's 66.2 % win / PF 1.96 on 7 months of M1 is self-reported and MT5 validation "in progress"). — [GitHub soloshun](https://github.com/soloshun/Quantitative-XAUUSD-Strategy); [Gitee wg722](https://gitee.com/wg722/xauusd-backtest); [GitHub andywarui/xaubot](https://github.com/andywarui/xaubot)
